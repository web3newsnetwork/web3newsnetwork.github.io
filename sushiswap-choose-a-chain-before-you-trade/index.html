SushiSwap: choose a chain before you trade

SushiSwap trades happen on a chosen blockchain, and the cost can include a swap fee and network fee. Check the quote and chain before you confirm.

The Chain Times DeskOct 23, 2026 · 15:33 UTC3 min read

SushiSwap lets you swap tokens on a chosen blockchain, but the chain affects what you can trade and what the transaction costs. The swap itself uses liquidity pools: shared token reserves that set an exchange rate as people trade. Your wallet needs to be on the chain holding the token you want to spend.

Start by checking which network your funds are on and whether the token you want is available there. SushiSwap’s chain and token choices help frame that first decision: select the network, then choose the token pair. The same token name can refer to separate assets on different chains, so check the network label as well as the ticker.

Which chain should I choose on SushiSwap?

Choose the chain where your funds already sit, if the token pair is available there. That usually avoids adding a separate cross-chain transfer to the trade. If the asset you want is on another chain, SushiSwap’s cross-chain option can move value between networks as part of the swap, but it adds steps and may add costs.

Chains also differ in their network fees. These are paid to process transactions on the blockchain, not the same thing as SushiSwap’s trading fee. A lower network fee can make a small trade more practical, while the available tokens and liquidity still matter. Thin liquidity means a trade can move the price more, especially if the order is large compared with the pool.

What does a SushiSwap trade cost?

Read the quote before you approve or confirm. SushiSwap’s swap screen separates the fee from the network fee and shows the expected amount you will receive. The trading fee pays for the swap route or pool; the network fee pays for processing. These amounts can vary by chain, pool and transaction, so the displayed quote is more useful than a single fee figure remembered from another trade.

Also check price impact, the change in price caused by your trade size, and slippage, the amount the final price may move before the swap completes. The first describes the quote’s effect on the market; the second sets how much the execution can differ before the trade fails. A large slippage setting does not make a trade cheaper. It allows a wider price change.

Before confirming, compare these parts of the quote:

  • Network: Is it the chain where your funds and intended token are?
  • Amount received: Does the quoted output make sense for the amount you are spending?
  • Swap fee and network fee: Review both; one does not include the other.
  • Price impact and slippage: Check whether the trade size or allowed price movement is too high.

Is a cross-chain SushiSwap trade different?

Yes. A cross-chain trade starts on one network and delivers the output on another. You must choose both the source and destination chains, then review the final amount and any fees shown for the route. The process can involve more than one transaction or network, so its total cost may not match a simple swap on one chain.

For most straightforward trades, using the chain where your funds already are is the clearest choice. Compare the full quote before signing: the token pair, expected output and both fee lines tell you more than the chain name alone.
