US secures seizure order for $1.2bn in Binance theft

US officials secured court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2bn of crypto stolen from Binance, as efforts to track and freeze the funds continue.

The Chain Times DeskNov 13, 2026 · 19:51 UTC2 min read

U.S. officials have court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, a move that could keep some of the funds from being laundered or moved beyond reach. The Justice Department announced the action on Nov. 12, saying U.S. agencies and private companies had already frozen more than $2.7 billion of the stolen assets, according to its announcement on the Binance theft.

How much was stolen from Binance?

The Justice Department says members of the North Korean military, whom the FBI calls TraderTraitor, stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance on or about Oct. 29. The FBI has attributed the theft to the group. The Associated Press reported the seizure action and said the theft was about $6.3 billion.

Has the government taken control of all $1.2 billion?

No. The Justice Department says a court authorized the government to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of the funds already frozen. The broader total frozen so far—more than $2.7 billion—includes joint work by U.S. agencies and private-sector firms. A freeze can block transfers, while a court-authorized seizure or restraint gives the government legal authority to pursue control of assets. The announcement does not say the money has been returned to Binance or to customers.

Where could the stolen crypto go next?

Investigators say efforts to trace, freeze and seize the remaining assets are continuing. The Justice Department says TraderTraitor actors are laundering funds through cryptocurrency bridges, which move assets between blockchain networks, as well as mixers and exchanges. Mixers are services that can obscure the trail of crypto transactions.

Last week, the FBI and partner agencies urged virtual-asset service providers to block transactions linked to wallet addresses the group is using to launder the Binance funds. The government says the response has so far frozen more than $2.7 billion, but it has not said how much of the stolen crypto remains unaccounted for.
