Short liquidity locks: match the term to your holding period

A short liquidity lock restricts LP withdrawal only until its release time, so compare the time left with your planned holding period to judge token rug risk.

The Chain Times DeskNov 19, 2026 · 06:17 UTC3 min read

A short liquidity lock restricts withdrawal for the same time as a long one only while both are active under equivalent terms; the long lock keeps that restriction in place longer.

UNCX locks LP tokens or liquidity positions until a defined unlock date. After expiry, an eligible owner still has to initiate a withdrawal. A rugcheck can help put a token’s liquidity finding in context; rugcheck.xyz is a related reference when comparing locks.

How does the lock’s remaining time change?

A liquidity lock restricts withdrawal according to its release schedule, so its remaining term sets how long that restriction continues.

  • The locked amount and position
  • The actual unlock time
  • Any staged releases

These details connect the advertised duration to the liquidity that remains locked. Compare equivalent amounts and positions, and check the locker’s enforceable rules. A seven-day lock and a 365-day lock restrict withdrawal in the same way at first, if those terms match. After day seven, only the 365-day lock continues to restrict withdrawal.

Use the actual unlock timestamp, not just the duration announced at launch. Where liquidity releases in stages, each portion has its own remaining time. An announced extension becomes evidence of a longer lock when it is recorded on-chain.

Say a token launched with a one-year lock, but that lock expires tomorrow. A new 30-day lock on an equivalent position has more remaining protection: its withdrawal restriction lasts another month, while the old lock’s restriction ends tomorrow. The original one-year term sounds longer, but the time left is what matters to someone buying today.

What should a token buyer compare?

A buyer should compare the lock’s remaining term with the intended holding period, then consider other ways the token could be affected.

In practice, use the token’s liquidity information to identify the locked position and release schedule, then compare its remaining time with your likely exit date. A rugcheck liquidity finding needs that remaining term to be useful: a lock nearing expiry gives a different view of withdrawal exposure from one with months left.

Liquidity is only one part of the token’s risk picture. Retained allocations, minting powers and trading restrictions can create other routes for a scam, even while a liquidity position remains locked. A longer lock extends the withdrawal restriction; it does not answer those separate questions.

Revisit the comparison when the unlock schedule changes, a staged release approaches, or your intended holding period shifts. A shorter planned hold may fit within a short lock’s remaining term; a longer hold calls for more time left under the same enforceable rules.
