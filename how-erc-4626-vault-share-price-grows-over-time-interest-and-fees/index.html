How ERC-4626 vault share price grows over time: Interest and fees

An ERC-4626 vault share price grows over time when interest raises totalAssets faster than fee shares expand totalSupply, lifting each share’s value.

The Chain Times DeskNov 27, 2026 · 23:41 UTC3 min read

An ERC-4626 vault share price rises when its assets grow faster than its share supply. The price is totalAssets divided by totalSupply, so yield accrues to the pool rather than appearing as extra shares in each depositor’s balance.

Morpho vaults are curated, single-asset pools that allocate deposits across lending markets and other supported yield sources. Morpho vaults add a concrete setting for seeing how pool-level accounting turns yield into a higher value per share.

What changes inside a vault as yield accrues?

Deposits and earned interest add to totalAssets, while the share supply changes only when shares are minted or burned. The resulting price is stated in underlying units per share.

  • Deposit: A user puts in the vault’s underlying asset and receives shares in return.
  • Borrower interest: Interest earned by the pool raises totalAssets. With no share mint or burn, totalSupply stays the same, so each share represents more assets.
  • Fee shares: Morpho vault fees are taken by minting shares to the fee recipient. This expands totalSupply and dilutes existing holders, so the quoted share price reflects value after that dilution.
  • Market loss: A loss can reduce totalAssets and lower the price per share.

Interest is computed when the vault is read; it is not stored as a separate growing share-price figure. ERC-4626’s convertToAssets function maps a share amount to its underlying asset value using the vault’s accounting.

What does that look like for a 1,000 USDC deposit?

Take an illustrative case with no fees or losses. At a price of 1.00 USDC per share, a 1,000 USDC deposit buys 1,000 shares. The deposit raises both totalAssets and totalSupply, while the price stays at 1.00.

Later, suppose borrower interest adds 50 USDC to the pool. TotalAssets has risen by 50 USDC; totalSupply and the depositor’s 1,000 shares have stayed fixed. The price is now 1.05 USDC per share, so those shares redeem for 1,050 USDC. The gain appears in the value of each share, not in a larger share count.

In a vault that mints fee shares, totalSupply also rises as fees are charged. The share price therefore grows net of those fees: a holder’s quoted price already includes the dilution from fee shares.

How can you use share price to read your position and yield?

Multiply your share balance by the current price to estimate its underlying value. To estimate annualized APY, compare prices at two dates and raise the ratio to the power of 365 divided by the number of days between readings, then subtract one. This assumes no deposits or withdrawals during that period; the result reflects yield after fees recorded in the price.

ERC-4626 vs rebasing tokens

ERC-4626 vaults generally keep a user’s share count fixed as the underlying value per share changes. Rebasing tokens instead change the token balance to reflect a holder’s share of the pool.

How to calculate APY from share price

Divide the later price by the earlier price, annualize that growth over the elapsed days, and subtract one. The calculation uses the net price change, including the effect of fee shares minted during the period.
