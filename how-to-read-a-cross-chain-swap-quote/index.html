How to Read a Cross-Chain Swap Quote

A swap quote is an estimate, not a promise: compare the amount you will receive, the fees around the trade and the protection against price changes.

The Chain Times DeskOct 22, 2026 · 14:57 UTC3 min read

A cross-chain swap quote estimates how much of one asset will arrive on another blockchain, after the trade and its costs. Start with the estimated amount you receive, then check what the quote includes and what could change before the swap completes. The source amount alone does not show whether one route offers better value.

What does the estimated output tell me?

Estimated output is the amount the service expects to send to your destination address. Compare it with the amount you will deposit, using the same destination asset and the same source amount. A route with a better exchange rate can still pay out less if its fees are higher.

Some quotes show the trade price separately from fees for sending funds in or out. Chainflip’s SDK, for example, labels its estimated trade price as excluding deposit and broadcast fees, and lists fees by type. The final amount you receive is the useful figure for comparing quotes, but read the fee breakdown to see how it was reached. For more detail on judging a native route, see chainflip. That distinction matters because costs on the source and destination chains sit outside the swap itself.

Which fees should I check?

Look for fees charged by the trading pools, the network, the quote provider and the destination chain. Chainflip’s protocol docs describe liquidity fees for swaps, a network fee, and an estimated fee to broadcast the payout. A broker, the service that arranges the swap, may also add a commission. Which fees apply, and how they are shown, depends on the service and route.

  • Trade fee: charged by the liquidity pools used to exchange the assets.
  • Network or broker fee: charged by the protocol or the service arranging the swap.
  • Deposit cost: the fee for sending your funds on the source chain, often paid from your wallet.
  • Payout cost: the estimated fee to send the received asset on the destination chain.

Check whether the displayed output already subtracts each fee. Adding a fee twice can make a quote look worse than it is; overlooking one can make it look better.

Can the final amount change?

Yes. A quote is a snapshot, while the swap may execute later, after the deposit has been confirmed. Prices and available liquidity can change in the meantime. Slippage means the difference between the expected trade price and the price at execution. Chainflip’s documentation describes minimum-output and price-slippage limits that can trigger a refund if a trade falls outside the user’s chosen bounds.

Before sending, check the source and destination networks, the exact token on each chain, the recipient address and the quote’s expiry or refresh terms. Then review the minimum amount you accept and where a refund would go if the swap cannot meet it. For most readers, the better quote is the one with a clear net payout and understandable protections, not simply the highest headline rate.
