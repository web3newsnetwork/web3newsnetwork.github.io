Track Bridge Transfers Through Final Settlement

A bridge transfer is not complete when tokens leave one chain: treasury teams need to match the source burn, destination claim and final wallet receipt.

The Chain Times DeskOct 5, 2026 · 05:35 UTC3 min read

Treasury teams should track a bridge transfer as settled only after the destination chain shows the expected funds in the intended wallet. A source-chain transaction can start a transfer without completing it. Polygon Support’s guidance on withdrawals describes tokens being burned on Polygon before they are released on Ethereum, with a separate claim step. That gap affects cash planning, wallet balances and reconciliation.

When is a bridge transfer settled?

A transfer is settled when the destination-side transaction succeeds and the funds reach the expected address. A submitted transaction, a source-chain confirmation or a “ready to claim” message marks progress, but does not by itself prove receipt. Polygon Support’s Portal instructions say users initiate the transfer on Polygon, wait for “Claim Tokens” to appear, then claim on Ethereum and pay that chain’s gas fee.

The steps vary by bridge and route. The linked comparison of Polygon Bridge withdrawal paths explains why two withdrawals may have different exit steps. For a treasury record, keep the source transaction hash and the destination claim hash together, then confirm the final token and amount on the receiving chain.

What should treasury teams record?

Keep one record for each transfer, with enough detail to follow it from instruction to receipt. Polygon Support says its Portal lists transactions and shows when a claim is available; that status can help operations staff find the next step, while the destination transaction confirms whether the funds arrived.

  • Route: record the source chain, destination chain, bridge, token and receiving address.
  • Source evidence: save the approved amount, source transaction hash and confirmed status.
  • Pending step: note whether the transfer awaits a checkpoint, proof or manual claim. A checkpoint is a record of Polygon activity submitted to Ethereum.
  • Settlement evidence: capture the destination transaction hash, received amount and final wallet balance.

Also record fees by chain. Polygon Support’s Portal guide says a Polygon withdrawal needs POL for the source transaction and ETH for the Ethereum claim. These costs belong to different steps, so a single fee estimate can hide what remains to be paid.

How should teams handle pending transfers?

Keep initiated or claimable transfers in a pending account until the destination receipt is confirmed. This avoids counting the same funds as available in both chains, or treating a source-side burn as proof that the destination wallet has been credited. Reconcile the transfer against wallet activity and the bridge record, using transaction hashes to tie each step together.

Polygon Support says a withdrawal cannot be cancelled after tokens are burned, and a pending claim can be completed later. So a delay may leave funds in transit without making them available to spend. Escalate records that lack a destination receipt, and document any follow-up claim separately. The practical rule is simple: track the transfer through the last on-chain step, then mark it settled.
