Why your wallet total can differ from token prices

Your wallet total is an estimate built from token amounts and quoted prices, so timing, price sources and which accounts are included can change it.

The Chain Times DeskOct 4, 20263 min read

Your wallet total can differ from token prices because it multiplies your token amounts by prices supplied by a data service, and those inputs may not match what you see elsewhere. A token’s displayed price is a quote, not a promise that every unit in your wallet could be sold at that price. The total is useful for a quick view, but it is an estimate.

MetaMask’s help guidance says portfolio displays can lag because of data indexing, chain changes or incorrect token pricing. A price chart and a wallet view answer different questions: one tracks a quoted market price, while the other estimates the value of a particular balance. This Poocoin guide to charts and wallet values gives more detail on how to read the two together.

How is a wallet total calculated?

A wallet total is usually the sum of each token’s displayed amount multiplied by its quoted price. If a wallet shows 10 tokens at $2 each, it may show $20 for that holding. The displayed amount comes from the blockchain; the price usually comes from a separate data source. MetaMask says it gets token price data from CoinGecko, which illustrates why two apps can use different feeds.

The total can also depend on what the app includes. One view might cover a single account and network; another might combine several networks or addresses. MetaMask’s guidance on viewing balances across networks says users can filter which networks appear. Check the selected account and network before comparing totals.

Why can the quoted price be different?

Prices move, and services may refresh at different times or use different markets. A chart may show the latest trade on one exchange or a price derived from a trading pool. A wallet may use an aggregated quote. In a pool with little liquidity—the available depth for trades—a small swap can move the price, and the displayed quote may not be available for a large sale.

That gap matters most for small or newly listed tokens. A price shown beside a balance can make the holding look valuable even when there is little trading activity. Treat it as a rough estimate unless you have checked what buyers are actually paying and how much can be traded near that price.

What should I check when totals do not match?

Compare the same account, network, token and time. Then separate a balance problem from a valuation difference. MetaMask advises checking a token balance against the relevant block explorer, a site that reads public blockchain records. Ethereum.org explains that wallets are interfaces to balances recorded on the blockchain.

  • Confirm the account and network in both views.
  • Check the token amount on the network’s block explorer.
  • Compare the price source and the time each app updated.
  • For a possible sale, check the amount a trade would return after fees and price impact.

Ethereum.org’s fee guidance explains that network fees vary with the transaction and network conditions, so a sale’s proceeds can also differ from the wallet’s displayed value. If the on-chain amount is right, a different total usually points to pricing or display choices. Use the wallet figure as a snapshot, not a guaranteed cash-out amount.
