US gets court authority over $1.2bn in Binance theft

The Justice Department says a court authorized action against more than $1.2 billion in crypto stolen from Binance, adding to funds frozen since the October theft.

The Chain Times DeskNov 15, 2026 · 06:39 UTC2 min read

US authorities have court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, the Justice Department said Thursday. That is part of a wider effort to freeze and recover funds from a theft the FBI attributes to North Korean hackers.

In its announcement of actions against North Korean cryptocurrency theft, the Justice Department said more than $2.7 billion of the stolen assets had been frozen through US government and private-sector efforts. The court-authorized amount is part of that total.

How much was stolen from Binance?

The FBI says North Korean military-linked actors stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance on or about October 29. The bureau calls the activity “TraderTraitor.” Binance is a cryptocurrency exchange.

The Justice Department said it worked with virtual asset service providers, or VASPs—businesses that handle crypto transactions—to interdict the stolen assets. It did not name the providers or break down how much each helped freeze.

Does the court order mean the money has been returned?

The Justice Department’s wording is that a court authorized it to “seize or restrain” more than $1.2 billion. That describes legal authority to take control of or restrict the assets. The announcement does not say the funds have been returned to Binance or its users.

The Associated Press report on the seizure says the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge in such cases. The judge then decides who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds.

What happens to the rest of the stolen crypto?

Efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets are continuing, the Justice Department said. It said TraderTraitor actors are still laundering funds through crypto bridges, mixers and exchanges. Bridges move assets between blockchains; mixers pool transactions to make their paths harder to trace.

The FBI, Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation have urged crypto businesses to block transactions involving addresses linked to the theft, according to the Justice Department. It did not say when the remaining funds might be recovered or how much of the frozen total could ultimately be forfeited.
