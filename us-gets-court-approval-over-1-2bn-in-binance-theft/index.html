US gets court approval over $1.2bn in Binance theft

The Justice Department says a court authorized seizure or restraint of more than $1.2 billion from the Binance theft, as efforts to freeze the larger haul continue.

The Chain Times DeskNov 14, 2026 · 19:49 UTC2 min read

The U.S. Justice Department says a court has authorized the seizure or restraint of more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance in a late-October attack. The department announced the action on Nov. 12, as efforts to freeze and recover the stolen funds continue, according to its announcement.

How much of the stolen cryptocurrency is affected?

The court-authorized amount is part of a larger pool of funds that authorities and private companies have frozen. The Justice Department says their combined efforts have frozen more than $2.7 billion so far, and that more than $1.2 billion of that has court authorization for seizure or restraint.

That distinction matters: the announcement describes legal authority to seize or restrain the funds, not a completed return of the cryptocurrency to Binance or its customers. The Associated Press reports that U.S. authorities typically file a forfeiture claim with a judge, who decides who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds.

Who does the FBI say carried out the attack?

The Justice Department says the theft happened on or about Oct. 29 and involved more than $6 billion in virtual assets. It identifies the attackers as members of the North Korean military, referred to by the FBI as TraderTraitor.

The FBI has previously attributed the attack to the North Korean hacking group TraderTraitor, the AP reported. The U.S. announcement does not describe how the attackers gained access to Binance’s assets, so the available details do not establish the method of the breach.

What happens to the remaining funds?

Authorities say the work to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the stolen assets is ongoing. The Justice Department says TraderTraitor actors continue to launder funds through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. Bridges move assets between different blockchains; mixers are services that obscure where cryptocurrency came from.

The FBI has asked virtual asset service providers to block transactions involving addresses linked to the stolen Binance funds, according to the Justice Department. The department has not said when the ongoing recovery effort will end or how much of the frozen cryptocurrency will ultimately be forfeited.
