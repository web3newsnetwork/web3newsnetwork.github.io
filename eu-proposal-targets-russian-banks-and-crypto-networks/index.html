EU proposal targets Russian banks and crypto networks

The European Commission proposed targeting 60 Russian banks and crypto networks in a new sanctions package, but all 27 EU states must approve it.

The Chain Times DeskNov 24, 2026 · 19:24 UTC2 min read

The European Commission has proposed a new round of Russia sanctions that would target 60 banks and crypto networks, adding pressure on financial channels the EU says can help fund Russia’s war in Ukraine. The Associated Press report says the proposal also includes restrictions on Russian teams and athletes competing under their country’s flag at events in the EU.

What would the proposal do to banks and crypto?

Under the proposal, all 60 targeted banks would face a transaction ban and be removed from SWIFT, the messaging system banks use for international payments. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said the package would also target crypto and payment networks in Russia and in third countries that help Russia evade sanctions.

The Commission statement on the 22nd sanctions package says a Crypto Sanctions Task Force has been launched to support that work. It does not name specific crypto platforms or describe how they would be targeted.

Has the EU adopted the measures?

No. The Commission presented the package on 23 November, but all 27 EU member countries must endorse it before it can take effect. The AP reports that approval can take weeks and the measures may change during that process. The proposed bank and crypto restrictions are therefore not yet in force.

What else is included in the package?

The Commission also proposed listing more than 70 ships in Russia’s shadow fleet, a group of vessels used to move Russian oil, and banning tanker vessels insured in Russia. Other measures would bar Russian teams and athletes from competing under the Russian flag at EU sports events, prohibit official Russian symbols at those events and restrict Russian sponsors’ payments to European sports and cultural groups.

The proposal would also bar Russian former combatants from entering the EU and stop Europeans from holding governing posts at companies in Russia. The Commission says the package is intended to increase pressure on Moscow to end its war in Ukraine. Its final scope will depend on what member states approve.
