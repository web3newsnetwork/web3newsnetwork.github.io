How to Compare Bridge Swap Quotes Before You Send

A bridge swap quote is a forecast, not a promise. Compare the destination amount after fees, its minimum output, timing, and route details before you sign.

The Chain Times DeskOct 15, 2026 · 16:11 UTC3 min read

Compare bridge swap quotes by checking what should arrive on the destination chain after fees, then weigh the minimum output, timing, and route details. The biggest displayed amount is not always the best choice: it may depend on a different slippage limit or omit a cost shown elsewhere. Across's Swap API documentation, for example, describes output as varying with fees, slippage, and exchange rates. For a plain-language map of the options, see acrossbridge’s main bridge and swap types. Compare quotes for the same tokens, chains, and amount so you can judge them on equal terms.

Which amount should I compare?

Start with the estimated amount of the token you expect to receive, not just the amount you send. A bridge swap may move value across chains and also exchange one token for another. That means the displayed result reflects more than the bridge leg alone.

Look for a minimum output as well as the estimate. LI.FI’s quote documentation distinguishes the estimated destination amount from toAmountMin, the minimum value its quote says the transfer will yield after slippage. Slippage is the change in a trade’s price while it executes. When two quotes show different estimates, compare their minimums too: a higher estimate with a lower minimum leaves more room for the final result to fall.

How do I compare fees and timing?

Check whether the quote shows fees as separate amounts or folds them into the destination estimate. Across’s fee documentation says the total fee is reflected in the difference between the deposit and the amount received, and its quote tools can break out fee components. Other quote formats may list costs separately. Add listed costs where needed, but avoid subtracting them twice if they are already reflected in the receive amount.

Compare the expected duration as a guide, not a guarantee. LI.FI’s quote response includes an estimated execution duration, while Across’s API documentation includes expected fill time for comparing routes. A faster estimate may be useful when time matters; for many readers, a clearer fee breakdown and a stronger minimum output are better reasons to choose a route.

What should I check before signing?

Before you approve or send, confirm that each quote uses the same amount, source token, destination token, and chains. Then check the wallet and route details against what you intended. Quote fields and route names vary by service, so read the transaction summary itself.

  • Destination token and estimated amount.
  • Minimum output and slippage setting.
  • Fees, including any separate network or service costs shown.
  • Estimated time and the route used to move funds.

Quotes are snapshots of a route’s current estimate; the displayed amount can change before execution. Choose the option whose minimum output and costs you understand, and refresh the quote if its details no longer match the transaction you are about to sign.
