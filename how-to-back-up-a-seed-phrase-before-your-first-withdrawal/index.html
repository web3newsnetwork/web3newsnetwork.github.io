How to Back Up a Seed Phrase Before Your First Withdrawal

Back up your seed phrase before moving crypto off an exchange: write it offline, restore the wallet, match its address and test a small withdrawal first.

The Chain Times DeskNov 28, 2026 · 01:34 UTC3 min read

Write your seed phrase offline, restore the wallet from that backup, and confirm its address before withdrawing crypto from an exchange. Once the funds leave the exchange, it cannot recover your wallet access. Anyone with the phrase controls the funds, and there is no reset option.

Prepare the backup before withdrawing

Start with a new wallet that holds no funds. You need its app or hardware wallet and paper or a metal plate. A seed phrase is a list of words that can restore wallet access; BIP39 phrases have 12 or 24 words from a fixed list of 2,048.

Aave staking is a later decision about assets in your wallet. Aave staking can put eligible assets into Aave’s staking mechanisms for protocol risk management and rewards, so it changes what you do with assets after securing access.

  1. Write every word in order on paper or metal. Copy the spelling and sequence carefully. Never photograph the phrase, type it into a document, or store it in the cloud.

    For a BIP39 English phrase, the first four letters uniquely identify each word. You can record those four letters per word on metal and still have a complete backup. Keep each word’s position clear.

  2. Store the backup away from the device. Keep it somewhere private and protected from damage, so access to the wallet device alone does not reveal the phrase.
  3. Restore the wallet from the backup. Reset or wipe the wallet, then use its restore process and enter the words in order.

    If you set an optional passphrase, record it separately and securely. It creates a different wallet; restoring with the seed phrase but without that passphrase can open an empty account.

  4. Compare the receiving address shown by the restored wallet with the address you recorded before wiping it. Match the full address, not just its first or last characters.
  5. Withdraw a small test amount from the exchange to the restored wallet’s receiving address. Use the same network the wallet supports and the exchange withdrawal screen specifies.
  6. Confirm the test amount has arrived and that the restored wallet still shows the same receiving address. Once both checks pass, send the rest of your crypto to that address.

Keep the phrase private after the test

The test checks that the exchange can send to the wallet and that your backup restores access to the same address. It does not replace the backup: keep the phrase offline and private after the funds arrive. Treat a request to enter it into a website, message, or support chat as a request for control of your funds.
