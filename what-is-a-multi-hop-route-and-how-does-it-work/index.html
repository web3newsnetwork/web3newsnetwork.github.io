What Is a Multi-Hop Route, and How Does It Work?

A multi hop route in crypto swaps sends tokens through intermediate pools, such as A to ETH to B, when deeper liquidity can improve your output.

The Chain Times DeskNov 28, 2026 · 05:58 UTC3 min read

A multi-hop route in a crypto swap sends tokens through one or more intermediate tokens or pools, such as A→ETH→B. It can give you more output than a direct swap when the intermediate pools have deeper liquidity than the direct pool.

A direct swap trades through one pool. A multi-hop swap links pools to reach liquidity that may be easier to trade against. Each hop has its own pool fee and price impact—the change in price caused by the trade—so extra hops can also reduce your final amount.

Intermediate pools can improve a same-chain swap

Say you want to trade a small-cap token for USDC, but their direct pool is thin. A route through WETH may work better if both the small-cap token–WETH pool and the WETH–USDC pool are deep. The route takes two swaps, and the amount you receive depends on both pools, their fees and their price impact.

  • Direct: token A → token B through one pool
  • Multi-hop: token A → WETH → token B through two pools

On one blockchain, these hops settle as one atomic swap: either every hop succeeds or the whole transaction reverts. Uniswap v3 represents a multi-hop path as a sequence of tokens and fee tiers, so each pool is specified along the way. Before swapping, compare the quoted output after fees and check which pools the route uses.

Cross-chain routes can leave you with an intermediate token

A cross-chain route may connect separate swaps and bridge transfers across different blockchains. A lifi bridge aggregator such as LI.FI bridge route can find a path for moving assets between chains. When reviewing a quote, check the source and destination chains, the route’s fees and expected time, and the token each leg uses.

Unlike a same-chain atomic swap, cross-chain legs settle separately. An early leg can complete before a later leg fails. You may then be left holding the intermediate token on the chain where that earlier leg settled, rather than receiving the token you wanted. The route may need a separate follow-up swap or transfer to finish the move.

A multi-hop route is not the same as a single direct swap, and a cross-chain route adds another distinction: its legs may not settle together. More hops can reach deeper liquidity, but the quote and the token held at each stage matter.

  • Direct swaps use one pool; multi-hop swaps link pools.
  • Each hop adds its own fee and price impact.
  • Same-chain swaps revert as a whole; cross-chain legs can settle separately.
