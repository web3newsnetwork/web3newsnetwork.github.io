DOJ gets court authority over $1.2B in Binance theft

The Justice Department has court approval to seize or restrain over $1.2 billion of Binance’s stolen crypto, within $2.7 billion already frozen so far.

The Chain Times DeskNov 17, 2026 · 08:18 UTC2 min read

The U.S. Justice Department says it has court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance. That amount is part of more than $2.7 billion already frozen through government and private-sector efforts, according to the Justice Department’s announcement on November 12.

How much was stolen from Binance?

The department says more than $6 billion in virtual assets was stolen from Binance on or about October 29. The FBI attributes the theft to members of the North Korean military it calls TraderTraitor. The Associated Press reported that the FBI put the theft at about $6.3 billion.

The FBI says the stolen assets were being converted mainly into ether, a cryptocurrency that runs on the Ethereum network. It warned that the assets could be moved through decentralized finance services and instant-swap services as part of further laundering.

What does the $1.2 billion court authorization mean?

The Justice Department says the court authorization covers more than $1.2 billion of the funds already frozen. Its announcement uses the terms “seize or restrain”; it does not say how much of the covered crypto has been seized, how much remains restrained, or identify the court that issued the authorization.

The department says efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the stolen assets are continuing. It says TraderTraitor actors are still laundering assets through crypto bridges, mixers and exchanges. A bridge moves crypto between blockchain networks; a mixer obscures the path of crypto transactions.

Will Binance or its users get the money back?

The announcement does not say when the assets might be returned or who will receive them. The Associated Press report notes that U.S. authorities typically file a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then decides who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds.

The Justice Department says it is working with virtual asset service providers, the FBI, the U.S. Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation to trace and intercept the stolen crypto. Its announcement does not provide a timetable for the next steps.
