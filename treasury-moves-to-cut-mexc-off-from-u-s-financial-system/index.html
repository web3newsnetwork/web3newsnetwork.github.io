Treasury moves to cut MEXC off from U.S. financial system

FinCEN ordered U.S. institutions to block transfers involving MEXC, citing $16 billion in alleged illicit flows and raising the stakes for the global exchange.

The Chain Times DeskNov 9, 2026 · 15:10 UTC2 min read

The U.S. Treasury moved to cut crypto exchange MEXC Global off from the U.S. financial system, saying it handled at least $16 billion in transactions tied to sanctions evasion and other illicit activity. The Treasury announcement on November 6 says the flows were linked to Iran, North Korea and Russia, as well as cybercrime.

FinCEN, Treasury’s financial crimes unit, issued the order under Section 9714 of the Combating Russian Money Laundering Act. It bars U.S. financial institutions from sending funds to or receiving funds from MEXC. The order takes effect 30 days after it is published in the Federal Register, according to Treasury.

What transactions did Treasury cite?

Treasury says the $16 billion moved between May 2021 and May 2026 and was connected to sanctions evasion, cybercrime and other illicit activity. It says nearly $1.5 billion was linked to Russian sanctions evasion, including $232 million in direct transactions with U.S.-, European Union- and U.K.-sanctioned entities.

The agency also says MEXC sent or received about $1 billion in direct transactions with more than 40 Iranian crypto exchanges during that period. It cited about $22 million in transactions involving Iran’s sanctioned central bank and the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, including its Qods Force.

Treasury says MEXC was still processing transactions with sanctioned Russian exchanges Garantex and Grinex as recently as June 2026. It also cites at least $26 million in transactions benefiting Russia’s A7 sanctions evasion network and nearly $200 million connected to the A7A5 stablecoin.

When does the U.S. transfer ban begin?

The ban starts 30 days after publication in the Federal Register, Treasury says. Until then, the order has not yet taken effect. Once active, U.S. financial institutions may not transmit funds to or from MEXC, and violations may lead to civil or criminal penalties.

The CoinDesk report on the order says MEXC told the outlet it was reviewing the order and had launched an independent review of its anti-money laundering and sanctions controls. The exchange said it continued to operate normally.

What does the order mean for MEXC users?

The order targets transfers involving U.S. financial institutions; Treasury’s announcement does not say that MEXC must close or that users outside the United States are barred from using it. The action could still affect the exchange’s ability to move money through U.S. institutions once the order takes effect.
