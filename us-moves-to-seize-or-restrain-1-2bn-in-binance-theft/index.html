US moves to seize or restrain $1.2bn in Binance theft

The Justice Department says courts authorized the seizure or restraint of more than $1.2 billion from the Binance theft, as efforts to trace the funds continue.

The Chain Times DeskNov 17, 2026 · 23:39 UTC2 min read

US authorities have court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, the Justice Department said Thursday. The action puts a large share of the stolen funds within reach of law enforcement, while efforts to recover more continue, according to the Justice Department announcement.

The department said the theft happened on or about Oct. 29 and involved more than $6 billion in virtual assets. US agencies and private companies have frozen more than $2.7 billion so far. The FBI attributes the theft to North Korean military actors it calls TraderTraitor.

Has the US recovered the full $1.2 billion?

No. The Justice Department says it has court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of the frozen funds. That wording means authorities can take control of the assets or prevent them from moving; it does not mean the money has been returned to Binance or its customers. The Associated Press report also notes that the US government and private sector have frozen more than $2.7 billion combined.

The seizure or restraint is part of that larger frozen amount, rather than an additional sum. The Justice Department did not say how much of the $1.2 billion has already been transferred into government-controlled wallets.

Who does the FBI say stole the Binance funds?

The FBI identifies the group as TraderTraitor and says it is made up of North Korean military actors. The Justice Department said the theft took place on or about Oct. 29. The FBI’s attribution is the government’s assessment; the announcement did not name individual suspects or describe arrests in connection with the Binance theft.

What happens to the crypto now?

Authorities are still tracing, freezing, seizing and seeking to forfeit the assets, the Justice Department said. It said the actors have moved funds through crypto bridges, mixers and exchanges. Bridges move assets between blockchains; mixers obscure where funds came from.

Forfeiture is the legal process for transferring property to the government. AP reports that, in such cases, the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who decides who has a legitimate claim to the funds. The Justice Department did not say when that process would conclude or how much could ultimately be returned to victims.
