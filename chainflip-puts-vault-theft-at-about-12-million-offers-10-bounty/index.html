Chainflip puts vault theft at about $12 million, offers 10% bounty

Chainflip says an attacker took 122.86 BTC alongside ETH and stablecoins, lifting disclosed losses to about $12 million; it offered a 10% bounty for most of the funds’ return.

The Chain Times DeskNov 23, 2026 · 16:17 UTC2 min read

Chainflip says an attacker took 122.86 bitcoin from its vaults on Nov. 21, adding about $7.1 million to a theft that also included ETH and stablecoins. The protocol’s message to the attacker lists the stolen amounts and offers a 10% bounty for the return of most of the funds. The Block reports the combined loss at about $12 million.

What Chainflip says was taken

Chainflip Labs lists four assets taken from its vaults. The amounts show how the theft extended beyond the initial loss reported from its Ethereum vault:

  • 122.86137042 BTC from the Bitcoin vault. The Block valued it at about $7.1 million at the time.
  • 520.2243399 ETH from the Ethereum vault.
  • 3,249,631.41 USDC and 758,141.33 USDT, two dollar-linked stablecoins.

The Block had previously reported that $4.86 million in ETH and stablecoins was drained from the Ethereum vault. Adding the Bitcoin taken brings the estimated total to about $12 million, according to the publication. The dollar value can change with crypto prices.

The offer and deadline

Chainflip Labs says the attacker can keep 10% of the proceeds if the rest is returned. Its notice asks for 110.57523337 BTC and 2,618.1 ETH to reach two specified addresses by 18:38 UTC on Nov. 23. If both transfers arrive by the deadline, Chainflip says it will treat the case as a “white hat recovery” and withdraw its reports and complaints to law enforcement agencies.

The offer is conditional; Chainflip’s notice does not confirm that any funds have been returned. The Block reports that the Ethereum proceeds had been sent through Tornado Cash, a crypto mixing service, while the Bitcoin remained at the address where it landed. Those details describe the funds’ reported movement, not a confirmed recovery.

What Chainflip has disclosed about the breach

Chainflip says it identified the mechanism behind the key exposure and took steps to prevent similar attacks. It also says it traced preparation to Tornado Cash funding on Nov. 11, a FLIP token purchase, a validator account registered on Nov. 20 and the server behind that account. These are claims in Chainflip’s notice; it does not explain the technical cause in detail.

The disclosed figures establish the scale of the theft, but the notice does not say when normal service will resume or how affected users will be repaid. Chainflip’s deadline sets terms for a possible return. It does not establish that the offer will be accepted.
