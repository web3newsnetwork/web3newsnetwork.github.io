US wins authority to seize $1.2 billion from Binance hack

US authorities have court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion tied to the Binance theft, while efforts to trace and freeze the funds continue.

The Chain Times DeskNov 14, 2026 · 05:13 UTC2 min read

U.S. authorities have court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, the Justice Department said Thursday. The order covers part of the funds frozen since the theft, and efforts to track the rest are continuing, according to the Justice Department’s announcement.

How much was stolen, and when?

More than $6 billion in virtual assets was stolen from Binance on or about October 29, according to the Justice Department. The FBI has attributed the theft to North Korean military cyber actors it calls TraderTraitor. The Associated Press report describes it as the largest crypto heist in history.

The $1.2 billion is not the full amount stolen. The Justice Department said U.S. government and private-sector efforts have so far frozen more than $2.7 billion. Of that total, the department has court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion. “Restrain” means limiting access to assets while legal steps proceed; the announcement does not say that all those funds have been transferred into government custody.

How are investigators trying to stop the money?

The Justice Department said it worked with virtual asset service providers, such as crypto exchanges, to freeze the stolen assets. The FBI, Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation are coordinating efforts to recover them.

Investigators say the TraderTraitor actors continue to move funds through crypto bridges, mixers and exchanges. Bridges move assets between blockchains, while mixers can make the path of funds harder to trace. In an October 31 alert, the FBI urged crypto services to block transactions linked to addresses associated with the theft.

Will the money be returned to Binance?

The Justice Department said work to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets is ongoing. Its announcement does not say how much has been returned or when that might happen. Forfeiture is the legal process for transferring property to the government; the Justice Department has not announced a final outcome for these funds.

The scale of the freezes shows that investigators and private crypto firms have blocked a significant share of the stolen assets so far. But the gap between the more than $2.7 billion frozen and the more than $6 billion stolen leaves a large amount still outside that reported total.
