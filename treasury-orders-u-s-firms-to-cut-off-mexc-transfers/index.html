Treasury orders U.S. firms to cut off MEXC transfers

FinCEN ordered U.S. firms to block transfers involving MEXC, citing at least $16 billion in alleged illicit flows and links to Russian sanctions evasion.

The Chain Times DeskNov 8, 2026 · 15:43 UTC2 min read

The U.S. Treasury has ordered financial firms to stop transferring funds to or from crypto exchange MEXC, saying it handled at least $16 billion tied to illicit activity. The Treasury announcement says the order targets MEXC’s access to the U.S. financial system and covers transfers involving cryptocurrency.

FinCEN, Treasury’s financial crimes unit, issued the order on November 6 under a law focused on Russian money laundering. It takes effect 30 days after it is published in the Federal Register, Treasury said. Until then, the transfer ban is not yet in force.

What does Treasury say MEXC did?

Treasury alleges that between May 2021 and May 2026, MEXC facilitated at least $16 billion in transactions tied to sanctions evasion, cybercrime and other illicit activity. That is the government’s allegation behind the order, not a finding that every transaction was criminal.

Treasury says nearly $1.5 billion of the activity was linked to Russian sanctions evasion. It alleges $232 million involved direct transactions with entities sanctioned by the U.S., European Union and United Kingdom. The agency also says MEXC processed transactions with Russian exchange Garantex and its successor, Grinex, as recently as June 2026.

Which transfers will the order block?

Once effective, the order bars U.S. financial institutions from sending funds to or receiving funds from MEXC. The ban includes crypto transfers, according to CoinDesk’s report on the order.

The action relies on Section 9714 of the Combating Russian Money Laundering Act, even though Treasury announced it as part of a campaign against Iran. CoinDesk reports the law applies to foreign institutions linked to Russian illicit finance. Treasury says its order formally rests on MEXC’s alleged Russian dealings.

How broad are the alleged Iran links?

Treasury says MEXC sent or received about $1 billion in direct transactions with more than 40 Iranian crypto exchanges between May 2021 and May 2026. It also alleges about $22 million in transactions tied to Iran’s central bank and the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps and its Qods Force.

The order applies to U.S. financial firms handling transfers involving MEXC. Treasury says violations may bring civil or criminal penalties. Its announcement does not say the order shuts down MEXC’s global exchange service.
