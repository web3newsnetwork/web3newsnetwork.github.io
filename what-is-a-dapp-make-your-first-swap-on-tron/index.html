What Is a dApp? Make Your First Swap on TRON

What is a dApp and how do you use one? A dApp runs actions through smart contracts; connect a TRON wallet, sign a swap and cover network fees.

The Chain Times DeskNov 28, 2026 · 12:46 UTC3 min read

A dApp lets you use a blockchain app by approving actions from your own wallet. Its smart contracts carry out those actions on the blockchain, so you sign transactions instead of handing your funds to a company.

What makes an app a dApp, and what does its website do?

A dApp has two parts: a front end you see, such as a website, and smart contracts on the blockchain that do the work. The website helps you choose an action and prepare it; the contract carries it out after your wallet signs.

On TRON, smart contracts run on the TRON Virtual Machine (TVM). A contract call uses Energy and Bandwidth, the network resources for processing transactions. When those run short, the network can burn TRX to cover the call.

The website is a front door, not the contract itself. If a dApp’s site goes down, the same contract can still be called through TRONSCAN if you know which contract and action to use. That distinction is useful when a site is unavailable, but it also means you should know what you are signing. A wallet approval lets a contract carry out the action shown on the signing screen.

How do you use a dApp on TRON for the first time?

On SunSwap, you connect TronLink, choose a token swap, review the details and sign it. The router contract handles the exchange through liquidity pools, and the tokens you receive go to your wallet.

  1. Install a wallet such as TronLink and set it up. Keep your recovery phrase private.
  2. Keep some TRX in the wallet. Contract calls use Energy and Bandwidth, or burn TRX when those resources run short.
  3. Open the dApp’s site and connect your wallet. Check that you are on the intended site before connecting.
  4. Choose the tokens and amount for your swap. Read the wallet’s signing screen to confirm the action, then approve it if the details match what you intended.

After signing, the contract processes the swap on TRON. You can check the transaction and resulting wallet balances. The site helps you set up the request, but the signed contract call is what makes the swap happen.

A dApp is therefore a front end paired with contracts that act on your signed instructions. For a first TRON swap, that means using a wallet you control, keeping TRX for fees and reading each signing screen before you approve.
