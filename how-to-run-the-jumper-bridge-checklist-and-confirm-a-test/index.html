How to run the Jumper bridge checklist and confirm a test

Jumper bridge checklist before a large transfer: compare the route, price impact and amount received, verify the address and approval, then confirm a small test arrives.

The Chain Times DeskNov 28, 2026 · 06:41 UTC3 min read

Jumper bridge users should check the bridge route, amount received, destination network and address, and token approval, then complete a small test on that same route before sending a large transfer. Jumper bridge gives the cross-chain context for this checklist: Jumper bridging compares routes through supported bridges and swaps so you can choose how tokens move between networks. A large sum magnifies price impact, and an address or token mistake can put the transfer at risk.

Use this list when you are preparing one large transfer and want to confirm the route and destination before committing the full amount.

Which Jumper bridge route and quote details should you check?

Jumper bridge quotes show the bridge used, estimated time and amount received, so review those details before choosing a route.

  • Bridge: Open the route details and note which bridge carries the funds. Routes may name providers such as Across, Stargate or Circle CCTP; the bridge matters because it identifies the path your transfer will take.
  • Price impact: Compare the large-transfer quote with a smaller quote on the same route. A noticeable change can show how much the larger amount affects the trade.
  • Amount received: Read the minimum received as well as the estimate. This gives you a concrete amount to compare with what you intend to send.
  • Time: Note the estimated arrival time. It helps you judge whether the route suits your needs and gives you a reference for the test transfer.
  • Destination: Confirm the destination network, address and token. Check the first and last characters of the pasted address against the one you intend to use; clipboard malware can replace an address while you paste it.
  • Approval: For an ERC-20 token, review the approval amount before signing. The approval transaction lets the router move tokens, so make sure the amount matches what you intend to authorize. Etherscan’s Token Approval Checker can show existing approvals and let you revoke them.

LI.FI documentation explains route composition and quote fields. Use the quote details to compare the bridge path and expected result, then check the destination details in your wallet before signing.

Why send a small test on the identical route?

A small test confirms the address, destination token contract and real arrival time before the main transfer.

Keep the test on the same route, with the same destination network, address and token. Wait until it arrives, then confirm that the received token is the one you expected. The test costs an extra fee, but it checks the details against an actual transfer rather than a quote alone.

Only send the rest after the test arrives as expected. If you change the route or destination, the earlier test no longer checks those details. A confirmed test and a reviewed quote leave you with a checked path before you move the larger sum.
