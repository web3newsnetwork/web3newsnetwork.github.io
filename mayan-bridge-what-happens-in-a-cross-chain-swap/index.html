Mayan Bridge: What Happens in a Cross-Chain Swap

Mayan Bridge combines a cross-chain transfer with a swap; learn what happens between networks, what to check in a quote and how to make a first transaction.

The Chain Times DeskOct 14, 2026 · 10:08 UTC3 min read

Mayan Bridge is a way to move crypto between blockchains and swap it for another asset in one flow. That can save a separate swap after the transfer, but the transaction still depends on the bridge route and the networks involved. Ethereum.org explains that blockchains do not connect directly; bridges provide a route for moving assets or messages between them.

If you want to send one asset and receive another on a different chain, mayan bridge is a cross-chain bridge and swap service that lets users move and swap crypto assets across blockchains in a single flow, according to its operator. The basic task is to choose what you send and where you want the result to arrive, then review the transaction before signing.

How does mayan bridge move assets between chains?

A bridge coordinates activity on two blockchains because one chain cannot directly update the other. Ethereum.org describes several common methods: a bridge may lock tokens on the starting chain and issue a matching representation on the destination chain, burn tokens on one chain and mint them on another, or arrange a swap between parties. The method depends on the route.

When the transfer also includes a swap, the asset you receive can differ from the asset you send. The route has to account for both steps: moving value across chains and exchanging it for the destination asset. This can be simpler than bridging first and making a second swap, but a single flow does not remove the need to check what you will receive.

What should I check before my first cross-chain swap?

Start with the details that define the transaction. The same token name can refer to assets on different chains, so confirm the source and destination networks as well as the token on each side. Then compare the quoted amount with the amount you expect to receive. A quote is the proposed result for the route; it may change before the transaction is completed.

  • Confirm the asset and network you are sending from.
  • Confirm the destination network and token you want to receive.
  • Check the expected output and any minimum amount stated for the swap.
  • Keep enough of the source chain’s fee token to submit the transaction.

Next, connect a wallet that holds the source asset and submit the transaction it presents for approval. Wallets ask for a signature or transaction approval to authorize an onchain action. After sending, wait for the bridge route to finish and check that the destination wallet received the intended asset. Cross-chain transfers can take time because each network processes transactions separately.

What risks come with a mayan bridge swap?

Bridges add technical dependencies to an ordinary token transfer. Ethereum.org lists smart-contract flaws, problems with the systems that verify transfers, and delays or failures during network congestion among the risks. Its guide also notes that bridge designs make different trade-offs in security, speed, connectivity and cost. For a first swap, use an amount you can afford to have delayed or lose, and check the route details before approving it.
