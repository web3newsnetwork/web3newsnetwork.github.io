US gets court order over $1.2bn in Binance theft

A US court order now lets authorities seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion from the Binance theft, while efforts to trace and recover the rest continue.

The Chain Times DeskNov 19, 2026 · 02:17 UTC2 min read

US authorities have court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, a major step in efforts to recover funds from the exchange’s October theft. The Justice Department’s announcement on Nov. 12 says the theft was carried out by members of North Korea’s military, whom the FBI calls TraderTraitor.

How much has been frozen, and how much is covered by the order?

More than $2.7 billion of the stolen assets has been frozen through combined US government and private-sector efforts, the Justice Department said. The court authorization covers more than $1.2 billion of that total. A freeze prevents assets from moving; the order allows authorities to pursue seizure or restraint. The announcement does not say that all of the authorized assets have been transferred to government control.

The FBI told the Associated Press that more than $1.2 billion had been seized. The Justice Department’s more detailed account describes the amount as court-authorized for seizure or restraint. It also says efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets are ongoing.

Who does the US blame for the Binance theft?

The Justice Department says members of the North Korean military stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance on or about Oct. 29. The FBI refers to the group as TraderTraitor. The department says the group is still laundering assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. Bridges move tokens between blockchains, while mixers can obscure where funds came from.

The announcement does not identify the specific assets covered by the order or say how much, if any, has been returned to Binance or other owners. The AP reported that US authorities typically seek forfeiture through a claim before a judge, who determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds. That process is separate from obtaining authorization to seize or restrain assets.

What happens to the remaining stolen funds?

Investigators and private-sector partners are continuing to trace the funds, the Justice Department said. The FBI has also urged crypto businesses to block transactions involving addresses TraderTraitor is using to launder the stolen assets. The agency issued that alert with the US Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation.
