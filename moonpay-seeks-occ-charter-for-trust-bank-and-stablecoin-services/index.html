MoonPay seeks OCC charter for trust bank and stablecoin services

MoonPay has asked the OCC to charter a national trust bank for custody and stablecoin services, a move that could place those plans under federal oversight.

The Chain Times DeskNov 22, 2026 · 18:49 UTC2 min read

MoonPay has applied to form a national trust bank for custody and stablecoin services, according to The Block’s report on the filing. The proposed bank, MoonPay National Trust Bank, would need approval from the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC), the US bank regulator.

What services does MoonPay propose to offer?

MoonPay’s public application, dated November 17 and received by the OCC on November 18, outlines three areas of work: holding assets in custody for clients, stablecoin services, and currency conversion as an agent. The application says the stablecoin work would cover issuing and redeeming tokens and managing the reserves backing them, according to the OCC filing.

The proposal does not name a stablecoin or specify in its public overview whether MoonPay would issue one itself or provide services for another issuer. The filing says more detail is in confidential parts of the application, including the business plan.

Who would the proposed bank serve?

The application describes MoonPay National Trust Bank as a non-depository bank, limited to trust company operations and related activities. That means the proposed bank is not set up to take customer deposits. MoonPay is asking for no federal deposit insurance, according to the filing.

The filing lists the proposed bank’s main office at 407 Lincoln Road in Miami Beach, Florida, and says MoonPay Inc would own it outright. It names MoonPay co-founder Ivan Soto-Wright as interim president and chief executive and says he would chair the board. A fifth board member is still to be chosen.

What would OCC approval change?

If the OCC approves the application, the charter would let MoonPay offer the listed trust and stablecoin services through a nationally chartered bank. The Block reported that the company is seeking one federal framework for those activities. The application is a request for permission; it does not mean the bank is approved or operating.

The OCC filing also marks the proposed institution as a Federal Reserve member bank, while stating that it is not seeking deposit insurance. Those details describe MoonPay’s application, not a final regulatory decision. The OCC has yet to approve the proposed bank.
