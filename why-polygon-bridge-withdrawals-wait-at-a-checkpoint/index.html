Why Polygon Bridge withdrawals wait at a checkpoint

A Polygon PoS withdrawal can wait at a checkpoint before Ethereum releases the tokens; check whether the burn is recorded or a claim is still due.

The Chain Times DeskOct 3, 20263 min read

A Polygon PoS withdrawal can appear stuck because Polygon must record the withdrawal in a checkpoint on Ethereum before the tokens can be released. A checkpoint is a batch of Polygon network activity submitted to Ethereum. After it arrives, the withdrawal still needs a separate claim transaction on Ethereum. So a pause can mean the process is waiting, or that it needs another action from you.

Polygon Support explains that the bridge burns the token representation on Polygon and unlocks the corresponding tokens on Ethereum. For a closer look at timing and fees, see this guide to Polygon Bridge withdrawal times and costs. The key distinction is whether your withdrawal is waiting for its checkpoint or has reached the claim step.

What does “waiting for checkpoint” mean?

It means the bridge has not yet marked your Polygon withdrawal as ready to claim on Ethereum. Polygon Support’s bridge guide says the withdrawal starts with a transaction on Polygon; its help pages tell users to monitor the transfer until a claim button appears. The checkpoint carries proof of the Polygon activity to Ethereum, where the bridge can verify that the withdrawal happened.

Check the transaction hash—the public record identifier—in the Polygon Portal’s transaction view or a block explorer. Confirm that the initial withdrawal transaction succeeded on Polygon. If the portal still shows the checkpoint as pending, another wallet signature will not make the checkpoint arrive sooner. Polygon’s status page reports whether its listed services have a known outage, but a green status does not confirm that a particular withdrawal has advanced.

What should I do after the checkpoint arrives?

If the checkpoint is complete, look for a claim or “Claim Tokens” action in the portal. Polygon Support’s withdrawal instructions say to switch the wallet to Ethereum, sign the claim, and pay Ethereum network fees. The claim is a separate transaction: the checkpoint records the withdrawal, while the claim asks the Ethereum bridge contract to release the tokens.

  • Check that the original withdrawal succeeded on Polygon.
  • Check whether the portal says the checkpoint is pending or the claim is ready.
  • If a claim is ready, make sure the wallet is on Ethereum and has ETH for the transaction fee.

Polygon Support also says a claim transaction can remain pending if its gas price is too low, Ethereum fees rise, or an earlier wallet transaction is queued. Check the claim transaction’s status before sending another one. Its support guide recommends retrying a dropped transaction through the portal; if the portal gives a generic error, Polygon says an out-of-sync indexer, wallet compatibility issue, or temporary RPC problem can affect the interface.

When is a withdrawal actually stuck?

A delay alone does not show that funds are lost. First identify the last completed step: the Polygon burn, the checkpoint on Ethereum, or the Ethereum claim. If the checkpoint has arrived but the claim action is missing or fails, save the transaction hashes and contact Polygon Support through its official support channel. Polygon’s status page can help identify a broader service issue, while the transaction records show what happened to your transfer.

The practical takeaway is simple: wait while the checkpoint is pending, then claim on Ethereum when the portal says it is ready. That two-step withdrawal takes longer than a deposit, but checking each stage tells you whether to wait or act.
