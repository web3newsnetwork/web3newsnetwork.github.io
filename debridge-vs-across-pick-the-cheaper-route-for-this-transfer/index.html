deBridge vs Across: Pick the Cheaper Route for This Transfer

deBridge vs Across: Across usually costs less for small, same-token moves; deBridge can win on large transfers or swaps. Compare live quotes by amount received.

The Chain Times DeskNov 19, 2026 · 18:53 UTC3 min read

After bridging before, you know the cheapest route depends on the transfer: Across usually costs less for a small or mid-size ETH or USDC move when the token stays the same; deBridge can win for a large transfer or a cross-token swap. For a route to Monad, the supported token pair also matters. The Monad bridge option changes which routes you can compare, so check that your chosen asset and direction are available.

What makes one quote cheaper?

Fixed fee. Across has no fixed per-order fee in the fee model described in its docs. Its total includes a relayer fee and an LP fee. Relayers front funds on the destination chain; their fee covers destination gas and capital costs. LP fees compensate liquidity providers. Across says the UMA optimistic oracle verifies settlement.

deBridge’s DLN fee docs describe a flat fee in the source chain’s native token, plus a small percentage fee per order. The quote also reflects the solver’s spread: the difference between the rate offered and the value delivered. The flat fee is the key cost on a small transfer. Say you send a small amount: that same fixed amount takes a bigger share of your funds than it would on a large order. On a large transfer, the fee becomes a much smaller share.

How the fee scales. Across’s gas and capital costs can make up a bigger share of a small transfer, while its LP fee varies by route and liquidity. deBridge’s percentage fee and solver spread scale with the order, but its flat fee does not. That is why the fee structure generally favours Across for small, same-token transfers, while the difference can narrow as the amount grows. Neither pattern guarantees the cheaper live quote.

Swap and route. Both services can quote cross-token routes, but a swap changes the amount delivered as well as the bridge fee. Compare the final amount of the token you want to receive, not just a fee line. The same applies to Monad: both Across and deBridge have announced Monad support, but a specific asset, direction or route may not be available. For a Monad bridge transfer, confirm the exact route appears in each service before comparing.

Which one fits your transfer?

Choose Across when you are moving the same token in a small or mid-size amount and the route is supported. Its lack of a fixed per-order fee tends to suit that case. Its relayer and LP charges still vary with gas, capital costs and route conditions.

Choose deBridge when you need a cross-token swap, are moving a large amount, or Across does not offer your route. The flat fee weighs less on a large order, and the solver’s quoted spread can make deBridge competitive. On either service, request a fresh quote before signing; fees change, and the amount received is the clearest comparison.

Are you moving the same token in a small or mid-size amount, or making a large transfer or swap? The first case usually favours Across; the second is a reason to compare deBridge closely.
