Visa adds Monad as a stablecoin settlement option

Visa has added Monad as a stablecoin settlement route for participating issuers and acquirers, offering another way to settle card obligations onchain.

The Chain Times DeskNov 13, 2026 · 11:59 UTC2 min read

Visa has added Monad as a network for stablecoin settlement, giving participating card issuers and acquirers another way to settle obligations with Visa. The Monad Foundation announced the addition on Nov. 10 in its release about joining Visa’s settlement program, which quotes a Visa executive.

Who can use Monad for settlement?

Participating Visa issuers and acquirers can use the network to settle in stablecoins. Acquirers are banks and payment firms that process card payments for merchants. The release describes Monad as an added option; it does not identify any specific issuers or acquirers that are using it.

Visa’s Dan Roesbery said in the release that the added network gives partners more flexibility and supports a multi-chain setup for stablecoin payments. The announcement does not say which stablecoins can be used on Monad.

How does settlement on Monad work?

The Foundation says blockchain settlement can move funds on weekends and holidays. It contrasts this with card settlement over banking rails, which it says has traditionally followed a five-business-day schedule.

Monad is compatible with the Ethereum Virtual Machine, the software environment used by Ethereum apps. According to the Foundation, businesses already using Ethereum-based custody, wallets and compliance tools can typically extend those integrations to Monad instead of building new ones.

The Foundation also says transactions on Monad reach finality in under a second. Finality means a transaction is treated as settled and cannot be reversed. Visa’s statement in the release says the network expands its settlement options; it does not give a launch date for particular partners or payment flows.

What privacy features are planned?

The Foundation says its Private Settlement feature is intended to keep commercially sensitive transaction details confidential while allowing institutions to share them with auditors and regulators. The feature is planned for the coming months and is not described as live in the release.

Unchained’s report on the addition also notes that the announcement names no supported stablecoins or participating card firms, and that Visa had not made a separate announcement as of its publication.

How large is Visa’s stablecoin program?

The Monad release cites Visa figures showing a $25 billion annualized stablecoin settlement run rate as of September 2026, roughly 10 times the level a year earlier. It also cites more than 225 stablecoin-linked card programs live worldwide. Those figures describe Visa’s broader program; the announcement does not say how much settlement activity will use Monad.
