EU proposes crypto crackdown in 22nd Russia sanctions package

The European Commission has proposed targeting crypto and payment networks linked to Russia, aiming to curb sanctions evasion in its 22nd sanctions package.

The Chain Times DeskNov 24, 2026 · 23:20 UTC2 min read

The European Commission has proposed new measures targeting crypto and payment networks it says could help Russia evade sanctions. The proposal is part of the EU’s 22nd sanctions package, presented on 23 November. The Associated Press report says the package still needs approval from all 27 EU member countries before it can take effect.

What would the crypto measures cover?

Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said the EU would crack down on crypto and payment networks, focusing on Russia and also on third countries that help Russia evade sanctions. The statement did not name specific platforms or countries, or set out how the proposed restrictions would work.

The Commission also said it had launched a Crypto Sanctions Task Force to support this work. Its statement describes the task force as part of efforts to strengthen enforcement and align measures with international partners. It did not give further details about the group’s membership or timetable.

How does crypto fit into the wider package?

The proposal also extends a transaction ban to about 60 Russian banks, which would be removed from SWIFT, the international messaging system banks use to arrange payments. The Commission presented the bank and crypto measures as steps to target ways Russia finances its war in Ukraine.

The package includes other proposed restrictions too. The Commission said it would list more than 70 ships in Russia’s “shadow fleet,” its term for vessels used to move oil outside normal restrictions. It also proposed banning Russian teams and athletes from competing under the Russian flag at sports events in the EU, and barring display of official Russian symbols at those events.

When could the proposal take effect?

It is not in force yet. According to the European Commission statement, the measures were presented as a proposal. The AP reports that all 27 member countries must endorse the package, a process that can take weeks and may change the planned measures.

For crypto businesses and payment providers, the proposal signals a possible expansion of EU sanctions enforcement beyond Russia to third-country networks accused of helping it avoid restrictions. The Commission has not yet identified which services could face measures. That detail, and whether member states will approve the package as proposed, remains unsettled.
