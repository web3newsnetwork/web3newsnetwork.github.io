Alta raises $47.5 million for bank-focused blockchain

Alta says its $47.5 million Series A will fund a blockchain built for banks, with privacy and compliance controls aimed at moving financial assets across borders.

The Chain Times DeskNov 11, 2026 · 17:32 UTC3 min read

Alta has raised $47.5 million in a Series A round to build blockchain infrastructure for financial institutions, the company said in its funding announcement. The system is designed to add privacy and compliance controls for banks and other regulated firms moving money and assets across borders.

Who led the $47.5 million round?

Index Ventures led the Series A, with GV (Google Ventures), Blackbird Ventures, Square Peg and Capital 49 also taking part, according to Alta. The company said it had previously raised $32.5 million in seed funding, bringing its total funding to $80 million. Fortune’s report on the raise said Alta’s valuation was not disclosed.

Alta’s chief executive is Loong Wang. Fortune reported that Wang started the company with co-founder Catherine Porter after Alta changed its name from Metal. The company says the new funding will support development of its blockchain, institutional partnerships, global financial connections, and growth in its engineering and compliance teams.

How would Alta’s blockchain handle private transactions?

Alta says its blockchain uses recursive zero-knowledge cryptography, a method for checking that a transaction follows rules without exposing the underlying details. The company says this can keep balances, transactions, identities and compliance policies private while still allowing the network to verify that transactions meet required rules.

Alta says privacy, compliance and identity controls are built into the blockchain. Fortune reported that the system is intended to let customers check whether transactions follow rules in different jurisdictions. Alta has not announced a launch date in its funding announcement.

What will Alta build first?

Alta says its first product will help businesses manage liquidity using tokenized assets, digital representations of financial assets recorded on a blockchain. The product is intended to support just-in-time funding, foreign exchange and movement into and out of yield-bearing tokenized assets, according to the company.

The goal is to reduce money held in prefunded accounts: cash that firms set aside in advance to cover payments. Alta says these balances can be tied up across countries and payment networks. It estimates that $4 trillion is held in such accounts globally, though the announcement does not cite the estimate’s source.

Airwallex has chosen to build on Alta, according to the announcement. The companies say the planned connection will link Airwallex’s payments network with Alta’s blockchain and support stablecoin payments as well as links to local currency payment networks in 200 countries and 90 currencies. The announcement describes these as planned capabilities; it does not say they are already available.
