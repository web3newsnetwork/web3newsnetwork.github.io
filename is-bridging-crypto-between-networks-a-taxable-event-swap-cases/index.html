Is bridging crypto between networks a taxable event? Swap cases

Bridging the same asset between your own wallets is often not taxable. Swaps, wrapped tokens and gas paid in crypto can change the result by country.

The Chain Times DeskNov 21, 2026 · 06:07 UTC3 min read

Bridging the same asset between wallets you control is often not taxable, because many tax systems treat it as a transfer rather than a sale. A swap, a new asset representation or a fee paid in crypto can change the answer.

The IRS treats digital assets as property, and its guidance says a transfer between your own wallets is generally non-taxable. HMRC says there is no disposal when you keep beneficial ownership, including when moving tokens between addresses you control. IRS guidance and HMRC’s disposal rules set out those principles.

Gnosis Bridge moves tokens and messages between Ethereum and Gnosis Chain, and Omnibridge can mint a representation of an asset on the destination chain. If you are considering the return leg, Gnosis Bridge’s bridge back from gnosis covers sending tokens back from Gnosis.

When does bridging crypto become a disposal?

A bridge route can become a disposal when it exchanges one token for a different one. HMRC counts exchanging tokens for a different type of token as a disposal; the IRS also treats exchanges of digital assets for other property as potentially taxable. The gain or loss generally depends on the value received and the disposed token’s cost basis.

  • Same asset, new chain: A transfer to another wallet you control usually carries your original cost basis forward.
  • Token swap: Trading ETH for xDAI may count as disposing of ETH, even if the route is presented as a bridge.
  • Bridge representation: A wrapped or bridged token may be treated as a new asset in some jurisdictions. The tax result can depend on whether the rules see it as the same asset or a token exchange.

Is wrapping ETH taxable?

Wrapping ETH may be taxable if your tax authority treats the wrapped token as a different asset received in exchange for ETH. The rules are unsettled in many places, so the fact that a wrapped token tracks ETH’s value does not settle its tax treatment. Keep the transaction record and ask an adviser how your jurisdiction treats that specific wrapper.

Can gas paid in ETH create a taxable event?

Yes, paying gas in ETH can itself count as disposing of that ETH. The IRS says a transfer between your own wallets is non-taxable except for digital assets used or withheld to pay transaction services. For a small gas payment, compare the ETH’s value when spent with its cost basis: that can produce a small gain or loss, even when the bridged token itself was not disposed of.

Does bridging DAI to xDAI count as a swap?

It depends on the route and the tax rules. In this illustrative case, ETH goes in and xDAI comes out through an aggregator such as Bungee or Jumper; because ETH was exchanged for xDAI, flag the ETH disposal. By contrast, the xDai Bridge converts DAI to xDAI at the same value, making it a closer case to a same-asset bridge move. Gnosis Chain’s documentation describes xDAI as generated when DAI is sent to the bridge, but HMRC’s token-exchange rule means the tax classification can still depend on the facts.

How to record cost basis for bridged tokens?

Record both chain legs, the amount and type of tokens sent and received, the wallet addresses, timestamps, fees and transaction IDs. Carry the original cost basis forward where the move is treated as a self-transfer; separately record any swap or ETH used for gas so an adviser can calculate a gain or loss. These are general principles across jurisdictions, not tax advice; check the rules that apply where you file.
