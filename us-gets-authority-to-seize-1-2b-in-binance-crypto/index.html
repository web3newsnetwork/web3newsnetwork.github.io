US gets authority to seize $1.2B in Binance crypto

The Justice Department won court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in crypto stolen from Binance, part of a wider recovery effort.

The Chain Times DeskNov 18, 2026 · 03:27 UTC2 min read

US authorities have court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, after a hack the FBI attributes to North Korean actors. The Associated Press report says the FBI announced the action on Thursday, Nov. 12.

The Justice Department’s Nov. 12 announcement describes the funds as part of a larger pool that authorities and private companies have frozen. It says those efforts have frozen more than $2.7 billion so far, and that the department obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of that amount.

How much was stolen from Binance?

The Justice Department says members of the North Korean military, whom the FBI calls TraderTraitor, stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance on or about Oct. 29. The FBI has attributed the theft to the group. The announcement does not say that the full stolen amount has been recovered.

“Seize or restrain” describes what the court has authorized the government to do with the assets. It is not the same as saying the government has already taken ownership of them. The Justice Department says work to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the funds is continuing.

Where did the stolen crypto go?

According to the Justice Department, TraderTraitor actors continue to move the stolen assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. Bridges move crypto between blockchains; mixers can make the path of funds harder to follow. The department says US agencies have worked with virtual asset service providers to stop the funds moving beyond law enforcement’s reach.

The announcement does not give a breakdown of the $1.2 billion by token, blockchain or seizure location. It also does not say how much of the $2.7 billion frozen total has been returned to Binance or its customers.

What happens to the funds now?

The court authorization allows the government to pursue seizure or restraint of the identified assets. The Associated Press reports that the government typically files a forfeiture claim before a judge decides who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds. The Justice Department says efforts to trace and forfeit the Binance assets remain underway.
