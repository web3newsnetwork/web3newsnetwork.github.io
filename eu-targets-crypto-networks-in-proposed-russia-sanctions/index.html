EU targets crypto networks in proposed Russia sanctions

The European Commission has proposed a 22nd Russia sanctions package that targets crypto and payment networks, with a new task force to support enforcement.

The Chain Times DeskNov 23, 2026 · 23:25 UTC2 min read

The European Commission has proposed targeting crypto and payment networks in a new round of sanctions on Russia, aiming to limit ways the country can finance its war in Ukraine. The proposal also covers 60 Russian banks and would launch a task force focused on crypto sanctions, according to The Associated Press.

How would the proposal affect crypto services?

Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said the package would crack down on crypto and payment networks. The measures would focus on Russia and also include third countries that help Russia evade sanctions, according to von der Leyen’s statement on the European Commission’s 22nd sanctions package.

The statement does not name specific crypto platforms, tokens or companies. It also does not spell out how the proposed restrictions on networks would work. The Commission said it had launched a Crypto Sanctions Task Force to support the effort. Von der Leyen described international coordination as a focus of the package.

What else is in the package?

The Commission proposes extending a transaction ban to about 60 Russian banks and removing all 60 from SWIFT, the messaging system banks use to communicate about international payments. The package would also list more than 70 vessels linked to Russia’s shadow fleet, according to the Commission statement.

Other proposed measures include visa bans for Russian ex-combatants and a ban on Russian teams and athletes competing under their national flag at sports events in the EU. The Commission also proposes restrictions on displaying official Russian symbols at sports and cultural events, and on financial flows from Russian sponsors to EU-based sports and cultural groups.

When could the measures take effect?

The package is a proposal, not an active set of restrictions. The Associated Press reported that all 27 EU member countries must endorse it before it can take effect. That process can take weeks, and the measures could change during negotiations.

For crypto users and firms, the proposal signals a planned expansion of sanctions enforcement into payment networks and services connected to Russia. The Commission has not yet published a list of affected crypto platforms or detailed how the restrictions would apply to them.
