CFTC chief says Hyperliquid must follow the same exchange rules

CFTC Chair Michael Selig said onchain venues need exchange approval or a registered partner to offer derivatives to U.S. retail traders, with no special rules for blockchain platforms.

The Chain Times DeskNov 22, 2026 · 03:27 UTC2 min read

Onchain trading platforms such as Hyperliquid must follow the same rules as other derivatives exchanges if they want to serve U.S. retail traders, CFTC Chairman Michael Selig said on Nov. 19. He said venues can register as exchanges or partner with firms that already hold registration, according to The Block’s report from the Capitol Crypto Forum.

“You're going to have to come in and partner with an exchange or obtain your own exchange,” Selig said, according to The Block. He added that platforms could keep using blockchain technology, but would still face the same rules as other exchanges. The comments describe the CFTC chair’s position on derivatives offered to U.S. customers; they do not announce a new rule specifically for Hyperliquid.

The two routes are already being pursued by crypto firms. Selig pointed to Backpack as an example of a company seeking its own U.S. entry. The Block reported that Backpack received three licenses earlier that week: one for futures brokerage, one to operate a derivatives exchange, and one for clearing derivatives trades.

Kraken parent Payward is pursuing the partnership route. It announced plans in September to offer perpetual futures through CFTC-regulated Bitnomial Exchange using Hyperliquid’s blockchain, subject to approval, CoinDesk reported. Perpetual futures are futures contracts without an expiry date.

CoinDesk also reported that Hyperliquid itself remained unavailable to U.S. customers. The distinction in Selig’s comments is between the technology a platform uses and the service it offers: operating on a blockchain does not, by itself, remove the exchange requirements for offering derivatives to U.S. retail traders.

That leaves platforms with a choice of seeking their own registration or working with a registered exchange. The reporting cited examples of both approaches, but the proposed routes do not mean Hyperliquid or any other platform has received approval to serve U.S. retail traders. Payward’s plan, in particular, remains subject to approval.
