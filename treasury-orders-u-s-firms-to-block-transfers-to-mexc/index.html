Treasury orders U.S. firms to block transfers to MEXC

FinCEN says MEXC handled at least $16.7 billion in likely illicit funds and orders U.S. financial firms to block transfers involving the crypto exchange.

The Chain Times DeskNov 9, 2026 · 23:02 UTC3 min read

The U.S. Treasury has ordered financial firms to stop transferring funds to or from crypto exchange MEXC, citing at least $16.7 billion in likely illicit activity. The order targets the exchange’s access to U.S. financial channels; it does not say MEXC’s assets have been seized. In its November 6 announcement, Treasury said the activity included sanctions evasion and cybercrime linked to Iran, North Korea and Russia.

What does the order prevent U.S. firms from doing?

FinCEN, Treasury’s financial crimes agency, issued the order under Section 9714 of the Combating Russian Money Laundering Act. It bars covered financial institutions from making transfers of funds to or from MEXC Global. The order also covers transfers involving the exchange’s overseas branches, subsidiaries and successor entities, while excluding any MEXC branches, subsidiaries or offices in the United States.

The restriction takes effect 30 days after publication in the Federal Register. Until then, the order has been issued but is not yet effective. The rule concerns financial transfers, including some crypto transfers. It does not itself order MEXC to close its platform.

What does Treasury say the $16.7 billion represents?

FinCEN’s published order says that between May 2021 and May 2026, MEXC facilitated at least $16.7 billion in funds connected to Russian illicit finance and other likely illicit activity. That is the agency’s estimate of flows over five years, not a report of money seized or currently held by the exchange.

The order says nearly $1.5 billion had direct or indirect exposure to transactions linked to Russian sanctions evasion. FinCEN also identified about $1 billion in direct transactions with more than 40 Iranian crypto exchanges. It says about $22 million was linked to Iran’s central bank and the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, a state military body that the U.S. has sanctioned.

Why is the action framed around Russian finance?

Treasury announced the move as part of Operation Economic Outcast, a campaign it says is aimed at cutting Iran off from global finance. But the legal finding behind this order is that MEXC is a money-laundering concern in connection with Russian illicit finance. FinCEN says the exchange processed transactions with sanctioned Russian exchange Garantex and its successor, Grinex, as recently as June 2026.

FinCEN also says MEXC handled about $1.99 billion in funds originating from or moving to illicit cyber actors during the five-year period, including scam and ransomware proceeds. These are the agency’s findings and allegations in support of the order. The documents do not establish that every transaction through MEXC was illicit.
