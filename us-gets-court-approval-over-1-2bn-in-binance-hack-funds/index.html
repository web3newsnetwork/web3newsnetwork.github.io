US gets court approval over $1.2bn in Binance hack funds

US prosecutors won court approval to seize or restrain over $1.2 billion in Binance hack funds, part of $2.7 billion frozen as recovery efforts continue.

The Chain Times DeskNov 15, 2026 · 19:46 UTC2 min read

The US Justice Department has court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, part of a wider effort that has frozen more than $2.7 billion. The Justice Department’s announcement says recovery work is still underway.

Has the US taken ownership of the $1.2 billion?

Not necessarily. The court has authorized the government to seize or restrain the funds, but that does not mean a final decision has been made on who owns them. The Associated Press reports that the government typically files a forfeiture claim, and a judge then decides who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds.

The distinction matters: the Justice Department says more than $2.7 billion of the stolen assets have been frozen through joint government and private-sector work. More than $1.2 billion of that total has court authorization for seizure or restraint. A freeze can stop assets moving while authorities pursue the legal process.

How were the Binance funds stolen?

The Justice Department says North Korean military members stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance on or about October 29. The FBI calls the activity TraderTraitor. In an FBI alert, the bureau attributed the theft to North Korea and said investigators saw the actors converting stolen assets mainly into Ether.

Authorities and private companies worked with virtual asset service providers, such as crypto exchanges, to block and trace the funds. The Justice Department says the hackers are still laundering assets through crypto bridges, mixers and exchanges. Bridges move assets between blockchains; mixers combine transactions to make tracing harder.

What happens next?

The Justice Department says efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the stolen assets are continuing. It did not say that all frozen funds have been recovered or returned to Binance. The AP reports that the theft was about $6.3 billion and says the FBI had previously linked it to TraderTraitor.

The seizure action was part of a broader Justice Department announcement that also covered a separate North Korean social engineering scheme called FaceStealer. The department says that scheme caused more than $400 million in cryptocurrency theft over the past 18 months. It is separate from the Binance theft.
