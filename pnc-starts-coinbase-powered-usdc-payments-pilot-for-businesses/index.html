PNC starts Coinbase-powered USDC payments pilot for businesses

PNC clients can receive USDC on Ethereum through Coinbase and have it converted into dollars in a bank account, in a limited corporate payments pilot.

The Chain Times DeskNov 10, 2026 · 14:44 UTC2 min read

PNC Bank has begun a limited pilot that lets eligible corporate clients receive USDC payments through Coinbase, then have the funds converted into dollars and deposited into a PNC account. The service gives businesses a way to accept stablecoin payments while keeping their funds in the bank, according to PNC’s announcement.

USDC is a digital token designed to track the U.S. dollar. PNC says clients can receive it on the Ethereum blockchain. Coinbase’s payment products power the service, which converts incoming payments at a one-to-one rate into U.S. dollars for deposit into a designated PNC account.

Who can use the USDC payment service?

The pilot is initially available on a limited basis to eligible clients of PNC’s Corporate and Institutional Banking unit. PNC did not say how many clients are taking part or when the service might become more widely available.

PNC says the service adds another way for businesses to receive payments alongside its existing faster payment options. It is designed to give clients faster access to funds and make cross-border transactions more efficient, though the announcement does not provide transaction times or costs.

What does Coinbase provide?

Coinbase supplies the payment infrastructure through its Payment APIs. PNC provides the bank accounts and treasury management services used to handle the dollars after conversion. The arrangement means clients can receive a payment in USDC and have the proceeds deposited in dollars at PNC, according to Coinbase’s account of the expanded partnership.

PNC says the pilot will help it and its clients evaluate how stablecoin payments could fit into business treasury operations. The bank described the service as a way to explore the technology, rather than announcing a full rollout.

How does this extend the banks’ earlier work?

The payment pilot builds on an existing relationship between the companies. Reuters reported that PNC had previously selected Coinbase’s crypto-as-a-service platform to offer crypto trading to bank customers, while PNC also agreed to provide banking services to Coinbase.

The new service moves that relationship into incoming business payments. For now, its reach is limited to eligible pilot clients; neither company announced a broader launch date.
