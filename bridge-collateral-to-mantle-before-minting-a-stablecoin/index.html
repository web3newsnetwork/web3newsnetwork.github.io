Bridge collateral to Mantle before minting a stablecoin

To mint a stablecoin on Mantle, first bridge an asset the lending app accepts, confirm it arrived, then check collateral rules, fees and repayment terms.

The Chain Times DeskOct 16, 2026 · 12:23 UTC3 min read

To mint a stablecoin on Mantle, bridge an asset the minting app accepts, then deposit it under that app’s collateral rules. Bridging moves value to Mantle; it does not mint the stablecoin by itself. The Mantle Standard Bridge guide describes deposits as a transfer followed by a relay, after which the balance can be checked on Mantle. For route details, see this guide to mantle bridge choices. The route matters because it affects which token arrives and how you can withdraw it later.

How do you bridge collateral to Mantle?

Start by checking the minting app’s accepted collateral list and the asset’s network. A token with the same name can have different contract addresses on different networks. The app needs the Mantle version it recognizes, not just a token that looks similar in your wallet.

Choose a route that supports that exact asset and destination. A bridge may lock tokens on the source network and release or mint a corresponding version on Mantle. Other routes may use a provider to move assets between networks. Fees, transfer times and withdrawal steps can differ. Mantle’s Standard Bridge guide also notes that its token support has limits, so check the route’s asset details before approving a transfer.

Send a small amount first if you have not used the route before. Once it arrives, confirm the token address and balance in your wallet or the app. Keep some MNT for Mantle network fees; the Mantle project’s network guide identifies MNT as Mantle’s native token.

How does bridged collateral become a stablecoin?

Deposit the arrived token into the minting app, then use its borrow or mint action. In a collateral-backed system, the app holds or accounts for your deposit and issues stablecoins against it. The deposit remains your collateral, while the stablecoins are a debt you may need to repay to withdraw it.

Read the app’s rules before confirming. They set which collateral counts, how much stablecoin it can support, and what happens if the collateral value falls. Some systems can sell collateral to cover a loan that no longer meets their requirements. This is separate from bridging: a successful transfer does not mean the asset qualifies for minting.

What should you check before minting?

Use the minting app’s own instructions for its current requirements. Check the asset, debt and withdrawal terms, and confirm the transaction details in your wallet.

  • Confirm the Mantle network and the collateral token’s contract address.
  • Compare the displayed deposit and mint amounts, including any fees.
  • Check the collateral limit and what could trigger a sale of your deposit.
  • Understand how repayment works and whether withdrawal requires another bridge transfer.

For most readers, the better route is the one that delivers the exact collateral token the app accepts and has clear exit steps. A cheaper or faster transfer is little help if its token cannot be deposited or is difficult to move back.
