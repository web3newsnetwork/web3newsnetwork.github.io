What zerofi Does When an XMR Transfer Is Pending

zerofi turns XMR into a token for Ethereum, but a sent Monero payment is only the first step; confirmation, processing and minting can all take time.

The Chain Times DeskOct 27, 2026 · 18:30 UTC3 min read

zerofi bridges Monero’s XMR to zXMR, a token designed to represent XMR on Ethereum, but sending XMR does not finish the transfer. Monero must record the payment, the bridge must process it, and the destination token must be issued. ZeroFi’s public project description identifies it as an XMR bridge that wraps XMR into an Ethereum token.

Those steps explain why a wallet can show “sent” while the bridge still says “pending.” In the transfer flow, zerofi names the route being discussed. The useful question is which step has finished, not just whether the payment left your wallet.

How does the zerofi XMR bridge work?

The bridge links two networks that do not share a ledger. You send XMR to a deposit address, then the bridge processes that deposit and issues zXMR to the Ethereum address you specified. The zXMR token is a wrapped asset: it represents value from another network in a form Ethereum applications can use.

That extra connection makes the transfer a sequence, rather than one instant exchange. The Monero transaction and the Ethereum token issuance each have their own network activity. A “pending” label can cover more than one of these stages.

Why is a zerofi transfer still pending?

A transfer may be pending because the Monero payment has not yet been included in a block, because the bridge is waiting for more confirmations, or because processing on the destination side is still underway. A confirmation means a transaction has been recorded in a block; bridges often wait for additional blocks before acting on it.

Check your Monero wallet first. If it shows the payment as unconfirmed, the wait is still on the source network. If it shows confirmations, compare the transaction details with the bridge’s status. Do not assume the zXMR is ready until it appears at the intended Ethereum address.

Timing depends on the current bridge settings and network conditions. Check the live transfer screen for its confirmation requirements and supported destination. A threshold shown by a bridge is a block count, not a promise of a fixed wait time.

What should you check before sending XMR?

Before starting, confirm the destination address and the minimum amount shown in the current transfer flow. Save the transaction ID, the reference used to identify your payment, so you can check the wallet record and report the transfer if it stops advancing. Make sure you have selected the Ethereum network and address where you expect to receive zXMR.

  • Sent, but unconfirmed: Check whether your wallet shows the transaction in a block yet.
  • Confirmed, but still pending: Check the bridge’s progress and stated confirmation requirements.
  • Marked complete, but no zXMR: Verify the destination address and that your wallet is showing the right network and token.

For most readers, the practical rule is simple: treat the XMR payment and the zXMR delivery as separate checkpoints. A pending status alone does not show that a transfer failed. If the status stops changing after the stated requirements are met, use the bridge’s current support channel and share the transaction ID; do not send a second payment as a test.
