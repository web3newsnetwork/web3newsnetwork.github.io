US wins court approval over $1.2B in Binance theft funds

The Justice Department says a court approved seizure or restraint of more than $1.2 billion in crypto from the Binance theft, part of $2.7 billion frozen so far.

The Chain Times DeskNov 16, 2026 · 21:06 UTC2 min read

The U.S. has court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, the Justice Department said Nov. 12. The assets are part of more than $2.7 billion frozen through joint government and private-sector efforts so far, according to the Justice Department’s announcement.

How much of the stolen crypto is under U.S. control?

The court-authorized amount is more than $1.2 billion. The Justice Department says that figure is part of the larger $2.7 billion frozen, and that efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the remaining stolen assets are ongoing. A freeze blocks assets from being moved; the department has obtained court authority to seize or restrain its share.

The FBI said the theft happened on or about Oct. 29 and involved more than $6 billion in virtual assets taken from Binance. The Justice Department described the attackers as members of North Korea’s military and said the FBI calls them “TraderTraitor.”

How are authorities tracing the remaining funds?

The Justice Department says the attackers continue to launder the stolen assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. Bridges move crypto between different blockchains, while mixers obscure the path of transactions. U.S. agencies are working with virtual asset service providers, or companies that handle crypto, to interrupt those movements.

The FBI, U.S. Secret Service and Internal Revenue Service’s criminal investigation unit issued an alert last week asking those providers to block transactions connected to addresses used to launder the Binance funds, the department said.

Does the approval mean the money has been returned?

No return to Binance or its customers was announced. The Justice Department says it has authority to seize or restrain the funds. The Associated Press reports that the U.S. government typically files a forfeiture claim, after which a judge decides who has a legitimate claim to recovered assets.

The government says the work to recover the stolen crypto continues. It did not give a timeline for a forfeiture claim or say how much of the frozen amount may ultimately be returned.
