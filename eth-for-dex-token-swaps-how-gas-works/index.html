ETH for DEX Token Swaps: How Gas Works

Why do I need ETH to swap tokens on a DEX? Each swap needs the chain’s native coin for gas, and a first token trade may need both an approval and a swap.

The Chain Times DeskNov 20, 2026 · 23:35 UTC4 min read

You need ETH to swap tokens on an Ethereum-based chain because every swap pays a network fee, called gas, in that chain’s native coin. The network uses that fee to run and process the transaction, so the token you are swapping cannot pay it.

  • Keep the native coin for the network your wallet is connected to.
  • Budget for an ERC-20 approval and the swap itself when needed.
  • Leave some ETH after swapping so your next transaction can run.

Why do I need ETH to swap tokens on a DEX?

A DEX swap is a transaction that the network must process. On Ethereum, Arbitrum and Optimism, that transaction needs ETH for gas. Validators or sequencers accept the network’s native coin as the fee; your token balance alone cannot cover it.

Fraxswap is an automated market maker, or AMM, for token swaps. Its embedded time-weighted market maker can execute large trades over time. The Fraxswap page explains how Fraxswap swaps work, including the role of its Router; a swap there still needs gas in the network’s native coin.

Can I pay gas with the token I’m swapping?

No. On these EVM networks, gas is paid in the native coin: ETH on Ethereum, Arbitrum and Optimism, or AVAX on Avalanche C-Chain. Keep the right coin on the same network as the token and swap. ETH on Ethereum, for instance, won’t pay a fee on Arbitrum.

Why does my wallet say insufficient funds for gas?

Your wallet may not hold enough of the network’s native coin to cover the transaction’s estimated fee. Gas cost depends on the gas used and the current gas price: more computation or a higher gas price raises the fee. Check that you have ETH or AVAX on the selected network, with enough left to cover the estimate.

Why does a first token swap need two transactions?

Many ERC-20 tokens require an approval before a DEX can use them. The approval is one paid transaction; the swap is another. So a first swap may require gas twice. Later swaps may not need a new approval, depending on the permission already granted.

Take a constructed example: you hold a token on Arbitrum and want to swap it through Fraxswap. You need ETH on Arbitrum for the approval, then ETH for the swap. The Fraxswap Router handles the swap instructions, but the network still processes and charges for each transaction. Keep enough ETH for both fees, plus a small buffer.

Can I swap my whole ETH balance?

Don’t spend the full balance if you still need ETH for gas. A swap of ETH into another token also needs gas, and leaving no ETH can prevent your next transaction. Keep a portion aside for future network fees.

What should I know next about ETH and gas?

How do I buy ETH and send it to my wallet?

Buy ETH through an exchange or a wallet’s supported purchase option, then send it to your wallet address using the network you plan to use. Match the withdrawal network to your destination; for Arbitrum swaps, you need ETH on Arbitrum.

Why is gas cheaper on Arbitrum than on Ethereum?

Arbitrum processes transactions through a sequencer and posts data to Ethereum, so its fee structure differs from Ethereum’s. Costs vary with network demand and transaction details, so Arbitrum fees can be lower without being fixed.

What is a gasless swap?

A gasless swap is one where you don’t pay the network fee directly in native coin. Another party or arrangement handles that cost. Check the wallet or service terms, since “gasless” doesn’t mean the network transaction has no cost.
