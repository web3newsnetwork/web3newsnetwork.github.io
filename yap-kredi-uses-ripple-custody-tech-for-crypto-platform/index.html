Yapı Kredi uses Ripple custody tech for crypto platform

Yapı Kredi is using Ripple’s custody system for its crypto platform, adding bank-based asset safeguarding as Türkiye requires platforms to use authorised custodians.

The Chain Times DeskNov 27, 2026 · 06:50 UTC2 min read

Yapı Kredi is using Ripple’s custody technology to safeguard digital assets for its crypto trading platform, Yapı Kredi Kripto, according to Ripple’s November 25 announcement. The system is installed on the bank’s own premises, the company said. The arrangement gives the platform a custody service: a system for holding and protecting customers’ crypto assets.

Ripple said Yapı Kredi Kripto will use Ripple Custody to manage digital assets and provide custody services. The announcement did not name the assets covered, disclose financial terms or give a launch date. It said Yapı Kredi received regulatory approval to offer crypto custody services in 2026.

The setup keeps the custody technology within the bank’s premises, according to Ripple. Crypto.news also reported that the bank deployed the system on-site, allowing it to maintain direct operational control over the infrastructure. Neither source provided technical details about how the system protects assets.

The partnership comes under Türkiye’s rules for crypto trading platforms. Ripple said the rules require platforms to keep at least 95% of customer crypto with an authorised custodian. The requirement makes custody infrastructure a core part of operating a platform in the country.

Türkiye’s Capital Markets Board introduced a regulatory framework for crypto asset service providers in 2024, according to Ripple’s announcement. Yapı Kredi’s approval to offer custody services followed in 2026. The announcement did not specify when the approval took effect or when Yapı Kredi Kripto began using Ripple Custody.

In its report on the partnership, crypto.news said Yapı Kredi’s move follows Ripple’s existing custody work with Garanti BBVA in Türkiye. Ripple described its system as the only custody technology used by leading Turkish banks licensed by the regulator; that description comes from the company, and the announcement did not name those banks.
