Choose a Fraxswap Pool When Token Risk Differs

How to choose a liquidity pool before providing liquidity: compare turnover, reserves and token risk, then start with an amount you can leave exposed.

The Chain Times DeskNov 18, 2026 · 18:34 UTC3 min read

Start with a pair whose tokens and price risks you can assess, because providing liquidity exposes you to both assets. To choose a Fraxswap pool, compare recent trading volume with the pool’s reserves, weigh the potential fee activity against token and withdrawal risks, then begin with an amount you can leave exposed.

Fraxswap pair contracts hold token reserves that swaps change. The basic idea is how Fraxswap pools hold tokens, with liquidity providers receiving LP tokens that represent their pool deposit.

Compare turnover, depth and token risk

Pool selection starts with the tokens you understand, then compares each pool’s trading activity with its reserves. TVL, or total value locked, is the value of assets held in the pool. Volume is the value traded over a chosen period.

Use the same time window for every candidate. Turnover equals volume divided by TVL for that period. To make a rough gross fee comparison per LP dollar, multiply turnover by the pool’s fee rate. It is a comparison, not a promise of returns: token prices can change, and fees depend on trading activity.

Say you are comparing two pools over the same day, with the same fee rate and tokens you consider equally risky. A pool with $1 million TVL and $100,000 in daily volume has 0.1 daily turnover. A pool with $4 million TVL and $200,000 in daily volume has 0.05. The first pool is smaller, but its volume is higher relative to its reserves, so its rough gross fee yield per LP dollar compares better at the same fee rate.

That does not settle the choice. Higher turnover may mean more fee activity per dollar, while shallower reserves can make trades move the price more. Compare those two cases with a deeper pool holding tokens you know well: its lower turnover may be outweighed for you by greater trade depth or a clearer view of token risks. Fees and activity vary by pair and over time.

Fraxswap also includes an embedded TWAMM, or time-weighted market maker, for executing large trades over time. When considering a pool in practice, focus on its reserves, recent volume and fee terms; the pair and its risks still matter to your deposit choice.

Make the first deposit a measured step

Your first deposit should match the amount you are comfortable leaving exposed to both tokens and pool conditions. Start by selecting a Fraxswap pair, and have a wallet with both tokens plus funds for transaction costs. Review the pair’s reserves and recent volume, compare its turnover and fee terms with another candidate, then confirm the deposit details in the interface.

If you are uncertain about a token or its price risk, choose a pair you understand and start with less capital. Before confirming, make sure you have checked the token amounts and the LP tokens you will receive. A pool’s past volume can help compare fee activity, but it cannot guarantee future fees or protect the value of your deposit.
