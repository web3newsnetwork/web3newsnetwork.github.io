How to Compare Cross-Chain Swap Quotes

Compare cross-chain swap quotes by what arrives, after fees, on the right chain and token. Check the route, minimum output and wait time before signing.

The Chain Times DeskOct 16, 2026 · 02:30 UTC3 min read

Compare cross-chain swap quotes by the amount you expect to receive on the destination chain after fees. A route that looks cheap at the start can deliver less once bridge charges, swap costs and network fees are counted. Check the same token, chains and input amount in each quote before choosing.

A relay bridge is one way to move value between chains: a relayer advances funds on the destination chain, then settles the transfer under the bridge’s rules. For more on the mechanics, read how a relay bridge moves funds. The route matters because two quotes can use different bridges, swaps or intermediate tokens, with different costs and timing.

Which number in a quote matters most?

Start with the estimated amount delivered, in the exact destination token you want. The 0x cross-chain cost guide explains that some costs are already reflected in the quoted buy amount, while others appear as separate fees. So a low displayed fee does not by itself mean a better deal. Compare the final output, then inspect the breakdown to see what is included.

Also check the minimum output. This is the least the route says it will deliver under its slippage limit, a threshold that can make a transaction fail if the market moves too far. A quote may show an attractive estimate but a much lower minimum. Uniswap’s quote API, for example, returns both an output amount and a minimum amount for relevant quote types. Treat the minimum as a useful measure of execution risk, not as the expected result.

What costs and route details should I compare?

Read the quote’s fee details and route description. 0x’s guide separates bridge and network costs, and notes that some costs are folded into the output amount. Uniswap’s API documentation says its quote response can include the proposed route and estimated gas fees. Interfaces show different levels of detail, so look for the total destination amount as well as any fee charged in a separate token.

  • Input and output: Confirm the amount, both chain names, and the full destination token name. Similar tickers can refer to different assets.
  • Fees: Check bridge charges, swap fees and gas on each chain. Confirm which are already deducted from the quoted output.
  • Route: Note whether the route swaps before or after bridging, and whether it uses an intermediate token. More steps can mean more places where execution depends on market conditions.
  • Minimum and timing: Compare the minimum output and the estimated wait. Across’s documentation describes relayer fees as part of the mechanism that can affect how quickly a transfer is filled.

How do I choose between close quotes?

Choose the route with the stronger combination of delivered amount, credible minimum and acceptable wait—not simply the smallest fee label. A slightly lower estimate may be worth taking if its minimum is closer to that estimate or its route is easier to understand. If the difference is small, refresh both quotes: they are snapshots, and prices or network costs can change before you sign.

Before approving, verify the destination chain and token in your wallet and check what permissions the transaction requests. The practical rule is simple: compare like for like, judge the full amount that arrives, and use the minimum and route details to understand what could change it.
