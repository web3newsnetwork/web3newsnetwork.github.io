EU proposal targets crypto networks in Russia sanctions package

The European Commission has proposed targeting crypto and payment networks in its 22nd Russia sanctions package, which still needs approval from all 27 EU states.

The Chain Times DeskNov 27, 2026 · 08:14 UTC2 min read

The European Commission has proposed targeting crypto and payment networks in its 22nd sanctions package against Russia, alongside measures affecting banks, energy and sport. The Associated Press report says all 27 EU member countries must approve the proposal before it can take effect.

In a statement on Nov. 23, Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said the package would extend a transaction ban to about 60 Russian banks and remove them from SWIFT, the messaging system banks use for international payments. She said the proposal also targets crypto and payment networks.

How would the crypto measures work?

The Commission says the measures would focus on Russia and also include third countries that help Russia evade EU sanctions. Von der Leyen said the bloc had launched a Crypto Sanctions Task Force to support that work.

The statement does not name specific crypto platforms or explain how the proposed restrictions would apply to them. The proposal was presented as part of a wider effort to limit ways of financing Russia’s war in Ukraine. The details may still change as EU countries consider the package.

What else is in the proposal?

The Commission also proposed listing more than 70 Russian shadow fleet ships, which the AP describes as vessels used to move illicit oil. It proposed banning tanker vessels insured in Russia from EU ports, and expanding restrictions on European energy services to cover electricity services and work on Russian energy infrastructure, including refineries.

Other proposed measures include visa bans for Russians who fought in the war against Ukraine. For sports events in the EU, the Commission proposes barring Russian teams and athletes from competing under the Russian flag, banning official Russian symbols and restricting Russian sponsors’ financial flows to European sports and cultural associations.

When could the sanctions take effect?

The measures are proposals, not rules already in force. According to the AP, all 27 EU member states must endorse them, a process that can take weeks. The package could be modified before approval. The Commission has not set an effective date in von der Leyen’s statement.
