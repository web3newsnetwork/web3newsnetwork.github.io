US wins court authority over $1.2B in Binance theft

The Justice Department won court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion from October’s Binance theft, as efforts to recover the funds continue.

The Chain Times DeskNov 15, 2026 · 18:52 UTC2 min read

The US Justice Department has court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, while efforts to trace and recover the funds continue. The department announced the action on November 12, saying the amount is part of more than $2.7 billion frozen through US government and private sector efforts.

What did the court authorize?

The Justice Department’s announcement says the court authorized the seizure or restraint of more than $1.2 billion. That does not mean the department has completed the recovery or returned the cryptocurrency to its owners. The department says work to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets is still under way.

Seizing or restraining funds can prevent them from being moved while the legal process continues. The Justice Department did not give a breakdown of the cryptocurrency covered by the court authorization or say when the process might be completed.

How much was stolen, and who does the FBI blame?

The Justice Department says more than $6 billion in virtual assets was stolen from Binance on or around October 29. It attributes the theft to members of the North Korean military, whom the FBI calls “TraderTraitor.”

More than $2.7 billion of the stolen assets has been frozen so far through combined government and private sector action, according to the department. The court-authorized amount is part of that frozen total. The department did not say how much of the original theft remains outside the freeze.

How are the stolen funds being moved?

The Justice Department says TraderTraitor actors continue to launder the assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. Bridges move digital assets between separate blockchains, while mixers make it harder to trace where funds came from by combining transactions.

In an alert issued the previous week, the FBI urged virtual asset service providers—companies that handle crypto transactions—to block transactions linked to addresses used to move the stolen Binance funds. The department says the FBI is coordinating recovery work with the US Secret Service and the IRS Criminal Investigation unit.

The seizure case is separate from another action announced the same day against “FaceStealer,” a North Korean social engineering scheme. The Justice Department says that scheme has stolen more than $400 million in cryptocurrency over the past 18 months. It also says the FBI seized more than 900 webcam videos of victims as part of the disruption.
