Coinbase brings Morpho-backed crypto loans to New York

Coinbase Borrow is now available to New York residents, who can borrow USDC against bitcoin, ether or solana through Morpho on Base.

The Chain Times DeskNov 22, 2026 · 16:12 UTC2 min read

Coinbase Borrow is now available to New York residents, completing its rollout across all 50 U.S. states, The Block reported on Nov. 17. Eligible customers can borrow USDC against bitcoin, ether or solana held on Coinbase.

Coinbase provides the app interface, while Morpho supplies the onchain lending infrastructure on Base, Coinbase’s blockchain network. “Onchain” means the loan activity is recorded on a blockchain. The New York launch followed approval from the New York State Department of Financial Services, according to a Coinbase spokesperson quoted by The Block.

New York borrowers start with three assets

The spokesperson told The Block that borrowing limits are up to $5 million against bitcoin, $1 million against ether and $100,000 against solana, subject to eligibility and other limits. The company did not provide application or approval dates, or details on the scope of the state’s approval.

Coinbase Borrow launched in January 2025. Since launch, it has originated more than $3.4 billion in loans, according to Morpho, as reported by The Block. Morpho’s own announcement of the New York launch said more than $1.6 billion was outstanding, backed by more than $2.8 billion in collateral. These figures describe loans already made and still active, respectively.

The product lets customers borrow without selling the crypto they use as collateral. Coinbase’s help page says rates can vary with supply and demand in lending markets. It also says collateral prices can fluctuate and protocol security or liquidity problems are among the risks. If collateral loses value, customers need to monitor their loan’s health to avoid liquidation, when collateral may be sold to cover a loan.

Other loan features remain unavailable in the state

New York customers can initially use bitcoin, ether and solana as collateral. The Block reported that fixed-rate loans and other collateral options, including tokenized gold, Zcash, Hyperliquid, XRP and staked assets, are not yet available there. Coinbase also has a separate USDC lending product, but that remains unavailable in New York, according to The Block.

Asked about bringing those features to the state, a Coinbase spokesperson told The Block the company was “excited to continue partnering with regulators to bring better financial services to New Yorkers.” The spokesperson gave no timetable for further additions.
