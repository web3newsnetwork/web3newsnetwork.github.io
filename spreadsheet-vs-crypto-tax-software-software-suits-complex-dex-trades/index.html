Spreadsheet vs crypto tax software: software suits complex DEX trades

Spreadsheet vs crypto tax software for recording DEX trades: software fits frequent swaps and LP activity; a spreadsheet suits a few dozen simple trades.

The Chain Times DeskNov 25, 2026 · 06:01 UTC3 min read

For frequent DEX trades, crypto tax software usually gives you more complete records; a spreadsheet can work for a few dozen simple swaps.

Trade count, LP activity and cost decide the tool

For a few dozen swaps across one wallet, a spreadsheet can be enough. You enter each trade yourself, which takes time and leaves room for missed rows. Software can import on-chain transactions by wallet address, so it suits higher volume and several wallets. Think of the import as a map of your wallet activity: it helps you find the route, but you still need to check that each stop is labeled correctly. The map analogy ends there; software does not guarantee a correct tax classification.

LP activity makes manual records harder. On shiba swap, a Dig deposit can import as two sales, while withdrawals and multi-hop swaps can also be mislabeled. Review those rows in any tool and reclassify them where needed. The shiba swap transaction record can help you identify the activity.

Software costs a subscription; a spreadsheet costs time on every trade and when you prepare records for filing. For an audit trail, keep dates, token amounts, values, wallet addresses, transaction IDs and fees. Record gas for each trade, including failed transactions, so the fee is not lost from the history. The tax treatment of a fee can depend on the transaction.

A missing wallet can leave out the original purchase of tokens later swapped from another wallet. That gap can affect the cost basis—the recorded value used to work out a gain or loss—for every later trade involving those tokens. The IRS treats digital assets as property, so swapping one asset for another can count as a disposal. A cost-basis method such as FIFO, which uses the earliest acquired units first, helps determine the gain.

Choose software if you trade more than a few dozen times, use LPs or bridges, or manage multiple wallets. Choose a spreadsheet if your swaps are few, simple and easy to check manually. Software still needs review: compare imports with wallet activity on Etherscan and correct missing or mislabeled transactions.

Related questions

How do I export transactions from Etherscan?

Open the wallet’s transaction history on Etherscan and export the available records. Check that the file includes token transfers and fees, then compare it with your tax tool or spreadsheet; an export may not label every DEX action correctly.

Are gas fees tax deductible?

Record gas fees, but do not assume every fee is a separate deduction. IRS rules treat some transaction costs as part of calculating a digital asset disposal. How a fee applies depends on what the transaction did.

Questions to ask next

Which cost basis method should I choose?

Use a method allowed for your situation, such as FIFO, and keep records that support how you identified the units disposed of. Ask a tax professional which method applies to you.

How do I record airdropped or reward tokens?

Record the token, amount, date received, wallet and transaction ID. Keep its value at receipt if you can establish it, and track later sales or swaps separately.

Do I need receipts for gas fees?

Keep transaction IDs and wallet records showing the fee, date and related activity. They provide a record even when there is no separate paper receipt.
